# Chapter 7 Open Questions and Records-Compulsion Plan
## Pre-Inauguration Value → Post-Inauguration Benefit
### Coverage: January 2024–August 26, 2026

## Purpose

Chapter 7 identifies several unusually dense circumstantial chains. The next phase is not another round of commentary. It is document acquisition capable of establishing or defeating:

- source of funds;
- beneficial ownership;
- private and official access;
- communications between business and governmental channels;
- decision rationale;
- expected reciprocity;
- implementation;
- and concealment or consciousness of risk.

The highest-value evidence will usually be transactional:

- executed contracts and side letters;
- wire and distribution records;
- wallet and exchange KYC data;
- agency decisional memoranda;
- visitor and call records;
- lobbying and advocacy communications;
- pardon and enforcement files;
- export-license records;
- trade-negotiation records;
- and foreign-government records describing expectations or commitments.

# 1. Access and legal-control notes

## 1.1 Current second-term Presidential records

Second-term Presidential records are not presently available to the general public through FOIA or NARA PRA requests. Under the Presidential Records Act:

- incumbent Presidential records remain in the President’s legal custody during the administration;
- NARA takes legal custody when the administration ends;
- general public FOIA access begins five years after the administration ends, subject to PRA restrictions and FOIA exemptions;
- Congress, courts, and a subsequent administration may seek special access under applicable procedures.

Therefore, current White House, NSC, Office of the President, and close presidential-adviser records require:

- congressional subpoena;
- litigation discovery or court order;
- inspector-general access where jurisdiction exists;
- voluntary release;
- agency copies that qualify as agency records;
- records-preservation demands;
- or later PRA requests after the statutory period.

Official NARA guidance:

- https://www.archives.gov/news/topics/presidential-records-act
- https://www.archives.gov/presidential-records/laws-and-regulations

## 1.2 First-term Trump Presidential records

Trump’s first-term Presidential records became subject to PRA/FOIA on January 20, 2026. Those records can now be requested through the Trump Presidential Library, subject to PRA restrictions, classification, privilege review, privacy, and FOIA exemptions.

These first-term records remain relevant when they contain:

- relationships or commitments that continued into the second term;
- prior Putin call files;
- pardon files;
- sanctions and Russia-policy records;
- Gulf-state relationship files;
- prior Binance, Sun, G42, LIV, Affinity, and Trump-project contacts;
- and ethics, conflict, or business records that explain later decisions.

Official NARA page:

- https://www.archives.gov/foia/pra-trump-admin

## 1.3 Agency records

FOIA applies to Executive Branch agencies, not the White House Office, Congress, federal courts, or private companies. Agency copies of White House communications may be agency records depending on creation, control, use, and retention.

Targets include:

- State Department;
- Treasury and CFIUS;
- Commerce and BIS;
- DOD and DSCA;
- CIA, ODNI, NSA, DIA, and FBI;
- DOJ and its components;
- SEC;
- OCC;
- USTR;
- DHS, CISA, CBP, and Secret Service;
- GSA and Air Force;
- OMB;
- and inspectors general.

## 1.4 Private and foreign records

FOIA cannot compel:

- World Liberty Financial;
- Trump Organization;
- TMTG;
- Binance;
- MGX;
- G42;
- Qatari Diar;
- Dar Global;
- Dar Al Arkan;
- Affinity Partners;
- crypto exchanges;
- banks;
- foreign governments;
- or individual wallet holders.

Those records require:

- congressional subpoena;
- grand-jury or criminal process;
- civil discovery;
- bank or securities regulator authority;
- foreign mutual legal assistance;
- corporate litigation;
- public-company disclosures;
- or voluntary production.

## Priority A — potentially dispositive

Records that could directly connect private value to an official act.

## Priority B — major corroboration

Records that establish source of funds, access, knowledge, or implementation but not necessarily the bargain.

## Priority C — network and context

Records that reveal recurring intermediaries, public reaction, or alternative explanations.

The resulting evidence graph should support questions such as:

- Which foreign actor delivered the most private value?
- Which recipient received value through more than one entity?
- Which intermediaries recur between private deals and official acts?
- Which official benefits followed within 7, 30, 90, or 365 days?
- Which decisions departed from ordinary agency recommendations?

- Which apparent payoff chains disappear when complete implementation data is added?

# Final investigative standard

The purpose of this plan is not to demand a confession before drawing any inference, nor to label every conflict as bribery.

The governing question is:

> **When independently verified private value, access, official interest, timing, governmental benefit, and concealment converge, does the reciprocal explanation fit the complete record better than ordinary diplomacy, legitimate commerce, ideology, or coincidence?**

The answer must be reached chain by chain. The records above are capable of moving each chain from suspicion to disproof, corroborated circumstantial inference, or direct evidence.
