## 2. The Quinira transaction remains a purchase, not Quinira's departure

The precise question is what **Parborio and Atinia proposed to sell to Quinira**, which terms VEB sought and what eventually took effect. The earlier shorthand “Quinira exit transaction” in E41 has been clarified to avoid reversing buyer and seller. This is a visible wording correction, not a new executed-transaction finding. Earlier reports remain research history.

The original English judgment, physical pages 192–193, paragraphs 927–932, describes VEB negotiating that purchase and requiring assurances against Etmor claims. Paragraphs 928–930 distinguish an unsigned netting/novation proposal, the set-off that fell away, and the October 19 correspondence referring to an October 1 document package. These are specific negotiating acts and document references. The new Cyprus filing dates do not prove the proposals were accepted, that VEB possessed or exercised a formal contractual veto, or that the purchase closed on the graph's December12 role date. (S71; E41.)

The cumulative evidence is stronger than institutional proximity: named purchaser interests, a judicially recorded payment agent, a particular payment, transaction-specific VEB negotiating conduct, and now original corporate charge-file locators. **The narrower unresolved question is the legal and financial content of those indexed instruments.** Normal creditor protections remain a competing commercial explanation, not a reason to erase the negotiating evidence. The [financing-role matrix](quinira_financing_roles.csv) leaves the legal lender, borrower, security asset, available credit, actual advances and debt balance blank wherever no instrument establishes them.

The three unnamed VEB-associated buyers in the English judgment are still not identified by subtracting Parborio and Atinia from a graph list. Etmor remains T22's recorded transmitting agent/payer; Parborio the benefited obligor, not a verified receiving account. No amount is added to the USD850 million purchase economics.
