# Source review corrections

## October 12 2026 personal criticism classification

An initial statement that 76 official records criticized Putin personally was too broad and has been withdrawn, together with the initial praise-versus-criticism balance and quarter comparisons derived from it.

A second source-context review accounted for all 76 originally negative-coded documents, without duplicates or omissions:

- 42 contain clear negative judgments of Putin or unambiguously his conduct, including mild disappointment and jointly attributed blame.
- 8 contain implicit or context-dependent criticism.
- 26 contain requests, conditional warnings, disagreements with claims, Russia-state criticism, or frustration with war/negotiation outcomes rather than clear personal negative evaluation.

This is a reclassification of the original 76 candidates, not a new exhaustive frequency finding. It does not establish that no negative language was missed elsewhere. Favorable language was also missed in the first pass, so no corrected praise-versus-criticism winner follows until both sides are reviewed symmetrically.

## Required comparison discipline

1. Distinguish direct personal/conduct evaluation, claim disagreement, bargaining pressure, requests, war condemnation and negotiation frustration.
2. Apply the same distinctions to allied leaders and to Putin.
3. Preserve countries, institutions and individuals as separate targets.
4. Preserve favorable conduct, affinity, trust, defense, peace intentions, call quality and ceremony separately. Do not compare a narrow praise definition against a broad adverse-rhetoric definition.
5. Preserve mixed statements and surrounding qualifications. Counting a document once does not describe the duration, emphasis or intensity of its rhetoric.
6. Label all review as AI semantic review; no human validation or measured inter-rater reliability is claimed.

Original component observations are retained for audit. They are first-pass research annotations, not an unqualified final comparative dataset. The revision ledgers supersede affected interpretations.

## Expanded-context amendment and new candidates

A later pass found additional Davos question-and-answer evidence in DCPD-202600046. The statement that Putin and Zelenskyy's hatred is bad for settlements meets the existing shared-blame criterion. Its conditional “stupid” remark is preserved separately, not converted into an unconditional assertion. The amendment changes the original 76-candidate distribution to **43 clear, 8 contextual, 25 separate**. The original review remains available alongside the documented amendment.

The favorable-language re-review also flagged 34 additional records for negative adjudication: **14 clear, 5 contextual, 15 separate**. Twelve of the 14 clear additions concern mild jointly attributed Putin–Zelenskyy shortcomings or settlement obstruction. Across the 110 adjudicated candidate records, the totals are therefore **57 clear, 13 contextual, 40 separate**. The other 133 of the 243 original Putin-personal candidate records are explicitly marked as not independently negative-adjudicated in this reconciliation. These numbers are candidate-audit accounting, not a new exhaustive frequency estimate or 57 harsh attacks.
