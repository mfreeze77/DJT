# Updating the archive

1. Create a new dated snapshot. Preserve prior manifests and annotation revisions; never relabel a live re-download as an old snapshot.
2. Enumerate the official DCPD indexes and available social indexes. Record requested pages, errors, collection timestamps, source cutoffs, hashes and source-specific gaps.
3. Keep raw downloaded material in an ignored private cache. Reassess rights before publishing any raw compilation or third-party media.
4. Normalize IDs as strings, preserve original timestamps and URLs, and retain every record even when excluded from analysis. Use Eastern dates for presidential event-day comparison and UTC timestamps for retrieval provenance.
5. Identify newly seen, edited, deleted, reposted or corrected records. Preserve exact source revisions and duplicate relations.
6. Build high-recall target candidates, including aliases, leaders, institutions and territory references. Manually/semantically review own-speaker attribution and contextual pronouns. Audit excluded candidate samples to identify false negatives.
7. Apply the versioned codebook consistently across targets. Store literal evidence, review agent/method, uncertainty and revision reasons. Human review is a separate status and must never be inferred from AI review.
8. Re-run validation, target-union aggregation and broad/strict sensitivity tables. Leave unresolved coverage or classification gaps explicit.
9. Link new source facts to relevant DJT ledger entries and prior evidence. Assess who benefits using a stated causal mechanism and observed versus expected outcomes. Do not convert rhetoric into an implemented policy without supporting records.
10. Prepare a reviewable change set for the user-confirmed repository. Publication of consequential new allegations requires the applicable approval. Do not merge automatically or revive a canceled write.
