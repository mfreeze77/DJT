# Research status and interpretation

Snapshot: October 12, 2026. This is a source archive and an auditable research work product, not a completed causal study.

## What is established by this package

- The retrieved official indexes contain 1,828 numbered DCPD packages, all preserved with source URLs and hashes. Source acquisition completeness refers to those indexes, not to all presidential speech.
- The portable social-source union contains 12,998 post identifiers: 12,855 Truth Social and 143 X. Coverage gaps, untranscribed media, repost attribution and source-specific reuse limits are recorded.
- The source archive can be restored and parsed offline. Structural tests check identifiers, coverage, quotation provenance and aggregation safeguards. They do not authenticate every assertion made by a speaker.
- First-pass target annotations and their corrections are retained. The original claim of 76 records criticizing Putin personally was withdrawn. Favorable-language review covers all 243 original Putin-personal candidate communications. These candidate records are not every opportunity to ask about Putin.
- The selective-restraint protocol separates press questions, follow-ups, volunteered remarks, prepared statements and social publication. It also separates responses from additional language the speaker chooses to introduce.

## What the annotations mean

Personal praise, praise of conduct, affinity, trust, defenses, statements about peaceful intentions and good-call descriptions are separate observations. Their union is not a count of personal compliments. Likewise, mild disappointment, joint Putin–Zelenskyy blame, personal insults, country criticism, requests and conditional threats must not be collapsed into one severity category.

Praise and criticism can occur together. Order matters: praise followed by criticism is different from criticism followed by a defense; a later passage in a long press conference is not automatically an immediate softening of an earlier answer. Mixed language also occurs about allied leaders.

All annotation and re-review in this release is AI semantic review. Literal quotation checks and independent AI review improve traceability but do not establish human validation or measured inter-rater reliability.

## What remains unfinished

1. A common, independently reviewed coding standard across all targets, with full severity and initiation coverage.
2. A complete inventory of comparable questions and response opportunities, including answers that omit the target's name, refusals and evasions.
3. Matched comparisons by topic, question, diplomatic setting and period; source-family and event-level deduplication across channels.
4. Independently sourced exposure-evidence classifications and a provocation register. No person is classified as having leverage merely because Trump speaks favorably about them.
5. Verification of policy implementation and outcomes against relevant primary records, separate from rhetoric.

The comparison CLI deliberately refuses first-pass, missing or unapproved review statuses. It must not regenerate an apparently final frequency table from withdrawn or asymmetrically coded observations. Correction ledgers supersede the affected first-pass interpretations.

## Cumulative interpretation

The rhetoric evidence can be joined to the repository's financial, access, political and policy records using source-linked event and person identifiers. Independent strands may strengthen a cumulative explanation; repeated reporting of one event does not create independent corroboration. An observed pattern of selective restraint would warrant explanation, while fear of exposure remains a hypothesis requiring independent evidence of the proposed mechanism. The research should retain findings that support, weaken or distinguish competing explanations.

## Initial question-versus-voluntary audit

The initiation audit reviews the historical 76 negative-candidate documents, with 123 annotated episodes. It retains the original review tiers and explicitly overlays the later Davos amendment. With the Davos amendment applied, 14 of the 43 clear-candidate documents contain at least one unsolicited critical passage and 29 contain only prompted critical passages. The 14 include 9 opening/speech settings and 5 pivots from other topics. Criticism is therefore not exclusively prompted. This selected subset does not establish the proportion of all Putin questions that receive criticism or the frequency of avoidance.

Five of those 43 documents contain clear criticism followed by affirmative favorable or excusing language after the last criticism in the same answer; two additional documents contain that sequence in a local oral speech passage. Favorable language interposed between critical statements is recorded separately. Sequence alone is not proof of retraction, intent or fear. The complete source-linked episode ledger preserves the questions, answer boundaries and alternative sequences.
