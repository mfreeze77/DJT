# October 21, 2026: OCC final GENIUS Act rule

## Verified change and implementation

The OCC issued its final GENIUS Act rule on October 21, effective **January 18, 2027**. It removes a proposed rebuttable presumption against specified issuer–affiliate/related-third-party yield arrangements, while retaining the issuer-yield prohibition and anti-evasion authority. This is a final agency rule with future effectiveness, not an individualized approval of USD1, World Liberty, Binance or an incentive program. [Announcement](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-92.html) · [Final original](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/news-releases/2026/nr-occ-2026-92a.pdf).

The [March 2 proposal](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/federal-register/2026/91fr10202.pdf), 91 FR 10288, proposed §15.10(c)(4)(i)–(iii), presumed prohibited yield where specified issuer-to-affiliate/related-party and onward-holder arrangements met its conditions. It included a brand-matching qualification for white-label arrangements and a written rebuttal process. It was **not a flat prohibition on every third-party reward**.

Final-rule pages 98 and 114–115 remove that screen. OCC explains that a presumption in a nascent market could be overinclusive, underinclusive and burdensome, and instead anticipates application review, ongoing supervision and case-specific action. Pages 101–104 retain anti-evasion authority, explain that agents paying on an issuer's behalf can fall within the prohibition, and reject automatic grandfathering of earlier programs. Pages 105–107 reject general and white-label safe harbors, among others; page 111 directs attention to economic substance when another stated purpose is merely nominal. Operative §15.10(c)(4) and (6), page 657, retain the issuer-yield and evasion prohibitions. **Removing the presumption does not create immunity.**

[October 21 application guidance](https://www.occ.treas.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-51.html) says acceptance of applications and waiver requests awaits OMB information-collection approval and a separate OCC announcement. Existing U.S. issuers with substantially complete pending applications by January 18 may seek transitional waivers; the OCC generally expects to grant qualifying prior-approval waivers absent stronger contrary reasons. This is neither automatic approval nor a blanket waiver of all requirements. The statutory ceiling is 12 months, and the agency expects duration limited to need. New/nonoperating issuers generally must await approval. BSA/AML/OFAC subjects are reserved for a separate coordinated rulemaking, not repealed by this rule.

## Existing evidence and significance

Event **J20-2026-034** and claim **CH7-GEN-OCC-001** join [CH7-UAE-USD1-001/002](Tmanch_CH7_claim_ledger.csv), [CH7-PAY-UAE-002](Tmanch_CH7_payoff_ledger.csv), and the [Binance incentive source note](Tmanch_CH7_Oct20_Binance_USD1_Incentives_Source_Note.md), source USD1-INC-S001. Separate SEC-dismissal and clemency entries CH7-BIN-SEC-001 and CH7-BIN-CZ-001/002 retain their own evidence and limits. No transaction total changes.

This adds a concrete regulatory-control choice to an existing commercial distribution/adoption channel: certain arrangements will not face the proposed burden of rebutting presumed prohibited yield. That is procedurally more permissive than the proposal, but no finding about a named program follows without its contracts, funding, direction and implementation records. The Binance notice advertises December 11 and January 8 payouts, both **before January 18 effectiveness**; this rule does not establish authorization for those earlier payouts.

[BitGo's published USD1 terms](https://www.bitgo.com/usd1-terms/), updated January 8, identify BitGo as issuer and World Liberty/SC Financial interests as brand/service providers (Definitions E). Issuer, brand, exchange and governance token are not interchangeable. These older terms do not independently establish subsequent charter implementation or issuer migration.

An industry-wide regulatory-calibration explanation has affirmative documentary support in the OCC's stated rationale and retained restrictions. Private commercial advantage is also a credible mechanism because affected issuer/distributor structures avoid the proposed presumptive burden. Neither conclusion establishes targeted favor, corrupt intent, quid pro quo or personal receipts; nor does the generic rationale resolve the separate family/government conflict question.

## Who benefits and limits

**National-benefit classification: mixed/uncertain, prospective.** Potential U.S. benefits include regulated dollar-payment infrastructure and entry under reserve/redemption safeguards; these are not measured results. Issuers/distributors may benefit commercially from reduced procedural friction, including the category in which USD1 operates. Weaker ex-ante screening and uncertain deposit/lending effects remain risks. The rule discusses uncertainty in these economic effects (pp111–114). No Russian-specific benefit, measured reserve growth, actual incentive payout, family receipt or individualized approval is established.

## October 22 historical source supplement: conditional charter, not completed migration

The OCC's August 14 [Corporate Decision 1385](https://www.occ.gov/topics/charters-and-licensing/interpretations-and-decisions/2026/cd1385.pdf), pp.1–2, grants World Liberty Trust Company, National Association preliminary conditional approval and documents its plan to assume USD1 issuance/custody from BitGo. This supplies a dated primary institutional plan beyond the older BitGo terms, not proof that the transfer occurred. Final authorization and preopening requirements remain explicit gates. Conditions include **$20 million minimum tier-1 capital**, separate liquidity covering 180 days of expenses, BSA/OFAC policies, and a preopening examination (pp.8–12). The capital minimum is a regulatory condition, not a payment or realized proceeds.

Condition 2 (p.8) also requires the bank to conform, cease or divest proposed activities as necessary for the GENIUS Act and future implementing rules. This links the conditional charter to J20-2026-034’s regulatory framework; it is not a special exemption.

This supplement does not change **J20-2026-034**'s October 21 date or establish final opening approval, issuer migration, reserve transfer, incentive authorization or personal receipts. Potential private benefit is prospective consolidation of issuance and custody; national effects remain mixed/uncertain rather than a Russian-specific benefit. The separate charter and general rule are not independent corroboration of realized commercial effects.
