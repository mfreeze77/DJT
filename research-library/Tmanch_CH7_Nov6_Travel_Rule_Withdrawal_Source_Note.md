**TRAVEL-WITHDRAW-S001:** [FinCEN's November 6 release](https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-announces-withdrawal-proposed-rule-related-recordkeeping-and-travel-rule) announces a joint withdrawal with the Federal Reserve Board of Governors. **TRAVEL-WITHDRAW-S002:** the [six-page public-inspection notice, FR Doc. 2026-22809](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22809.pdf), identifies the instrument as withdrawal of the joint NPRM published October 27, 2020 at 85 FR 68005. The release and notice are one institutional action, not independent proof of an economic outcome.

## Scope and chronology

The proposal being withdrawn would have reduced from $3,000 to $250 the thresholds for collecting/retaining information and transmitting information through the payment chain for covered transfers beginning or ending outside the United States. It also proposed clarifying relevant definitions to expressly cover domestic and cross-border convertible-virtual-currency and legal-tender digital-asset transactions. The notice describes the existing $3,000 framework; withdrawing this proposal does not repeal the current Recordkeeping or Travel Rule, exempt crypto businesses, or establish that existing CVC obligations disappear. [S002, PDF pages 1–3]

The PDF cover schedules publication for **November 9, 2026**. Its DATES clause still makes withdrawal operative as of the inserted Federal Register publication date. The footer records filing on **November 6 at 8:45 a.m.**, with no timezone printed. **TRAVEL-WITHDRAW-S003:** the [Federal Register public-inspection page](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-22809/threshold-for-the-requirement-to-collect-retain-and-transmit-information-on-funds-transfers-and), acquired November 8, explicitly says the document is unpublished and scheduled for November 9. ## Reasons, alternative explanation and benefit limits

The agencies give two primary reasons: more than five years have elapsed, so further public input would be useful before similar amendments; and CVC markets and legal frameworks have changed. The notice cites the GENIUS Act and the July 2025 White House digital-assets report/EO 14178 as context. This supports a documented administrative explanation, not a finding of personal presidential intervention. [S002, PDF page 5]

The notice summarizes commenter concerns about burdens, remittances, privacy, exposure from holding personal information and the adequacy of supporting analysis; comments on CVC clarification were mixed. These are attributed submissions, not measured costs or proven enforcement consequences. Potential benefits to institutions and users from avoiding proposed additional compliance obligations remain prospective/unquantified. The existing framework remains the baseline. No measured national, Russian, issuer or Trump-family benefit, additional illicit flow or quid pro quo is established. [S002, PDF pages 3–5]
