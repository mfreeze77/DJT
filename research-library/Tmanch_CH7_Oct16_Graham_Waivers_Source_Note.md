# October 16 Graham Act waivers: scope and implementation

Reviewed October 17, 2026. Supports **J20-2026-025** in the [event index](J20-Pres.md) and [structured ledger](Tmanch_CH7_J20_event_ledger.csv). This is a new presidential instrument, separate from enactment and earlier OFAC licensing.

## Primary order

The [White House order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/10/implementing-the-lindsey-o-graham-sanctioning-russia-and-iran-act-of-2026/) is dated October 16. Its full official indexed text was inspected; direct page retrieval failed.

- Section 4(a) waives sections 112–113 duties, including the order's tariff increases.
- Section 4(b) waives section 102(d)'s statutory maintenance requirement while expressly preserving the specified existing executive-order sanctions.
- Section 4(c) waives sanctions/restrictions under sections 102(a), 102(e), 103–111 and 117.
- Section 4(d) sets no calendar expiry: presidential or State Department action, with specified consultation, governs continuation.
- Sections 3 and 5 retain monitoring and delegate implementation/waiver authorities.
- Trump states that certifications and explanatory reports were submitted to Congress; their contents were not obtained.

## Statutory baseline and legal limits

[Public Law 119-111](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ111/html/PLAW-119publ111.htm), enacted September 18, already includes section 115 waiver authority. It requires advance written national-interest certification and an explanatory report; an unclassified report may include a classified annex. Exercising that authority is distinct from repealing the statute.

Section 117 addresses **termination**, including a Ukraine-accepted peace agreement and cessation of hostilities/subversion for Russian-person/Russia relief. Its congressional review normally lasts 30 days, with a seasonal 60-day exception. Subsection (c)(6) recognizes each chamber's constitutional rulemaking power. The order expressly targets section 117, but the legal reach of that waiver is not adjudicated here. Do not infer that all congressional oversight has disappeared, or that a termination certification was satisfied. The separate section 201 Iran Sanctions Act extension to 2031 is outside the listed waivers.

Transaction-specific compliance and other applicable laws still require assessment.

## Beneficiaries, competing explanations and cumulative assessment

**Analytical inference:** Foregone additional trade and financial pressure favors the Russian state and covered commercial actors, and potentially third-country counterparties otherwise exposed to the waived measures. Ukraine and allies lose a prospective source of bargaining leverage. This concerns avoided restrictions, not measured new revenue or a payment to anyone.

Preserving commercial flexibility could serve energy-market or negotiating aims; the public record here does not establish the underlying national-interest analysis, a reciprocal Russian concession, or realized U.S. consumer savings. The earlier IEA capacity constraint belongs to the diesel analysis and should not be extrapolated into an estimate of this entire order's effect.

Read alongside J20-033–035's earlier threats, J20-037 and -040's countervailing pressure, and J20-2026-023/-024's European and Ukrainian positions. The new decision strengthens the implementation-side evidence of Russia-favorable relief without establishing motive, coordination, personal benefit or unlawful conduct. It is one event, left unscored pending comparative review.

## October 18 source review: Russian envoy's reception

[RDIF identifies Kirill Dmitriev](https://www.rdif.ru/Eng_About/) as its chief executive and the Russian president's special envoy for investment and economic cooperation. His [Telegram post 735](https://t.me/kadmitriev/735) describes the Trump administration's action as a “важный и по-настоящему исторический момент” (important and truly historic moment), arguing that Russia–U.S. economic cooperation serves both countries and replacing sanctions with cooperation supports stability and peace. These are his policy assessments, not verified economic effects.

[TASS's October 17 report](https://tass.ru/politika/28224653) quotes and links that same post; it corroborates attribution and dates the reaction, but is not an independent policy or effects source. [Post 737](https://t.me/kadmitriev/737) calls Senate critics “Разжигатели войны” (warmongers) while reproducing their objections. Those quotations are not used here to verify the senators' original statements. The original channel text was inspected on October 18 through its [public context page](https://t.me/s/kadmitriev/735); precise publication times and post 737's date were not independently resolved, and no archive snapshot was retained.

**Analytical significance:** This adds contemporaneous reception by a Russian presidential economic envoy to **J20-2026-025**: he publicly presented avoided sanctions as an opportunity for bilateral cooperation. His description of a suspension does not replace the section-specific legal analysis above. The posts establish his framing, not a reciprocal Russian concession, actual receipts, U.S. motive, a private instruction or policy causation. No new event, score, financial amount or relationship is added.
