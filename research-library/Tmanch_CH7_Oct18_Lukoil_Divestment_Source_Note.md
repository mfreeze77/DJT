## Corporate chronology: disclosure is not closing

Lukoil announced a nonexclusive agreement with Carlyle on January 29 for its wholly owned LIG subsidiary. Kazakhstan assets were excluded and regulatory approvals, including OFAC permission, were conditions. The release gives no price or completed closing (LIG-S001).

The preliminary accounts state that the January agreement's validity lapsed at the end of July because OFAC had not approved it, with other buyers still in negotiations at preparation (page 29, Beilage I/22). This is the company's account of contractual status; neither the signed agreement nor OFAC's application record was inspected. Do not convert nonapproval by expiry into a finding of a formal rejection on a specified date (LIG-S002).

The same page describes a separate February 23 contract and March 31 OFAC permission for LUKOIL Mid-East Limited, through the Dutch upstream holding company in the LIG group, to transfer its 75% West Qurna-2 interest to Basra Oil Company. The underlying license, price and closing evidence were not obtained. This company-disclosed permission is not a license to sell the entire LIG portfolio (LIG-S002).

The later filing date does not change the earlier dates of the events it describes. No extra presidency-action rows are created for the corporate agreement, lapse or Iraqi contract/permission disclosure.

## GL 131J: authorization to negotiate, separate permission to perform

The September 18 instrument supersedes GL 131I dated August 20. Its October 22, 2026 expiry is **12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 UTC)**. Paragraph (a) covers specified E.O. 14024-prohibited negotiations and contingent contracts for LIG and entities it owns 50% or more; performance needs separate OFAC authorization. Paragraphs (b)–(c) cover specified maintenance/wind-down and associated blocked-account use. Paragraph (d) preserves other exclusions and bars transfers to persons or accounts in Russia (LIG-S003).

The FAQ includes due diligence among negotiation activities. For a future sale authorization, OFAC states expectations of complete separation from Lukoil, blocking amounts owed to it in a U.S.-jurisdiction account until sanctions lift, and no upfront windfall, including asset/share swaps. It also anticipates review before purchasers make further material disposals. These are announced review conditions, not an identified buyer's approved payment terms (LIG-S004).

The October 15 retail/Bulgaria extensions expire April 24, 2027 and are separately documented in [J20-2026-019's source note](Tmanch_CH7_Oct15_Lukoil_Licenses_Source_Note.md). They do not extend GL 131J's negotiation deadline.

## DFC–IHC framework: documentary context only

DFC's January 14 announcement describes a proposed agreement signed by Ben Black and Syed Basar Shueb in Abu Dhabi, with IHC chairman Sheikh Tahnoon present. Energy infrastructure is among the framework's sectors. The announcement names no Lukoil project, allocated investment, ownership percentage or project-specific financing commitment. It establishes an announced cooperation framework, not Lukoil financing or approval (LIG-S005).
