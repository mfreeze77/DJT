## Corporate chronology: disclosure is not closing

Lukoil announced a nonexclusive agreement with Carlyle on January 29 for its wholly owned LIG subsidiary. Kazakhstan assets were excluded and regulatory approvals, including OFAC permission, were conditions. The release gives no price or completed closing (LIG-S001).

The preliminary accounts state that the January agreement's validity lapsed at the end of July because OFAC had not approved it, with other buyers still in negotiations at preparation (page 29, Beilage I/22). This is the company's account of contractual status; neither the signed agreement nor OFAC's application record was inspected. Do not convert nonapproval by expiry into a finding of a formal rejection on a specified date (LIG-S002).

The same page describes a separate February 23 contract and March 31 OFAC permission for LUKOIL Mid-East Limited, through the Dutch upstream holding company in the LIG group, to transfer its 75% West Qurna-2 interest to Basra Oil Company. The underlying license, price and closing evidence were not obtained. This company-disclosed permission is not a license to sell the entire LIG portfolio (LIG-S002).

The later filing date does not change the earlier dates of the events it describes. No extra presidency-action rows are created for the corporate agreement, lapse or Iraqi contract/permission disclosure.

## GL 131J: authorization to negotiate, separate permission to perform

The September 18 instrument supersedes GL 131I dated August 20. Its October 22, 2026 expiry is **12:01 a.m. EDT (04:01 UTC)**. Paragraph (a) covers specified E.O. 14024-prohibited negotiations and contingent contracts for LIG and entities it owns 50% or more; performance needs separate OFAC authorization. Paragraphs (b)–(c) cover specified maintenance/wind-down and associated blocked-account use. Paragraph (d) preserves other exclusions and bars transfers to persons or accounts in Russia (LIG-S003).

The FAQ includes due diligence among negotiation activities. For a future sale authorization, OFAC states expectations of complete separation from Lukoil, blocking amounts owed to it in a U.S.-jurisdiction account until sanctions lift, and no upfront windfall, including asset/share swaps. It also anticipates review before purchasers make further material disposals. These are announced review conditions, not an identified buyer's approved payment terms (LIG-S004).

The October 15 retail/Bulgaria extensions expire April 24, 2027 and are separately documented in [J20-2026-019's source note](Tmanch_CH7_Oct15_Lukoil_Licenses_Source_Note.md). They do not extend GL 131J's negotiation deadline.

### Trident: a disclosed option predating the portfolio-sale process

Atash, **LIG-E011 / KvK 34282528**, states that it is wholly owned by **LIG-E003**. Its page 5 describes an April 8, 2024 farm-out agreement giving **MOL RBS Operator, LIG-E012**, an option over **57.8 percentage points of Atash's 87.8% Trident interest**, with the operator role. This is not 57.8% multiplied by 87.8%. Romanian government approval on the mineral-resources agency's proposal was a condition; the company says it applied June 25, 2025 and the review timeframe was unknown at signing (LIG-S007).

No option consideration, expiry, exercise, committed acquisition finance or completed transfer is established here. The filing's separate appraisal-work-program date of October 3, 2026 is not established as the option's expiry or the concession's termination. The option predates the October 2025 Lukoil designation and the January 2026 Carlyle announcement. It therefore cannot be attributed to those later events merely by sharing a Lukoil counterparty.

MOL's 2025 subsidiary appendix supplies the Hungarian counterparty's group-ownership context (LIG-S008). The nested table reports group interests; successive 60% multiplication is inappropriate. It does not resolve the option's present status or the identity/rights of the minority investor.

### Atash financing: distinguish instrument, facility and carrying balance

Page 12 identifies **PPL-003-2019**, signed August 21, 2019, with **LIG-E003 as lender and Atash as borrower**. The facility limit is **US$750 million**; outstanding principal at December 31, 2024 is **US$725.461 million**. It is indefinite, uses SOFR plus a revisable normal rate initially 1.6%, and limits interest by profit, with no interest when loss-making. The filing classifies the profit-participating instrument as **equity** under its accounting-policy choice (LIG-S007).

Separately, the filing describes ultimate-parent loans with a **US$381 million facility**, **US$76.592 million utilized**, maturity **December 31, 2029**, and **SOFR +4%** interest. The noncurrent-loan carrying balance is **US$103.852 million**; the note identifies accumulated interest reclassified long-term. It does not provide a complete bridge for every carrying-balance difference. These are source-reported obligations at year-end 2024, not acquisition proceeds or proof that a buyer financed Atash (LIG-S007).

Do not add facility limits to draws, principal to its carrying liability, or the equity-classified PPL again to equity/share-premium aggregates. Treatment on a disposal remains unknown: the inspected material does not establish assignment, forgiveness, conversion, repayment, escrow or distribution. The named creditor gives a specific reconciliation target, not a completed money trail.
