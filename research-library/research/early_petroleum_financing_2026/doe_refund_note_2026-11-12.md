# Conlo Service: bounded DOE refund-proceeding chronology

Reviewed November 12, 2026. This companion follows **HEF-0053** and its **RF236-1** award continuation. It does not establish a corporate identity match to either DTA 801597 petitioner or Macchia’s Conlo, Inc. See the [separate identity note](identity_note_2026-11-12.md). No ownership, acquisition, Panama, Russian-benefit or Trump-funding match follows.

## Sources and stages

- **1981 consent notice (CH2-P47):** The June 1 notice reports execution/effectiveness March 20, 1981 (notice issued May 11). It expressly states neither admission by Conlo nor finding by ERA of a pricing violation. This is a published summary of significant terms, not the acquired signed consent instrument. The $20,022 commitment over twelve months comprises $10,112 and $5,056 for two end-user classes ($15,168 together), plus $4,854 retailer-refund principal payable to DOE. End-user refunds could be checks **or credit memoranda**. These are components of one commitment, not additive new flows.
- **1985 proposed procedures (CH2-P48):** Issued May 31 and published June 10, the proposal responds to ERA’s October 13, 1983 petition. It reports $5,615.95 remitted to DOE, comprising $4,854 principal plus $761.95 interest, held in escrow. This is affirmative agency remittance reporting, not independently acquired banking proof. The $13,390.34 alleged-overcharge figure is an allocation denominator, not cash remitted or an adjudicated damages award. Proposed customer allocations were conditional; applications were not yet authorized.
- **1985 final procedures (CH2-P49):** Issued December 13 and published December 20, the final decision adopts procedures after no adverse comments. It reports that end-users received direct refunds, without acquired payee-level checks, credit memoranda or reconciliation. Twelve reseller allocations remained subject to claims and eligibility, not twelve verified payments. The final table’s whole-dollar potential refunds sum to $5,615 versus the $5,615.95 fund; its rounding footnote does not establish a missing transfer.
- **1986 Jimmy’s award (CH2-P50):** The July 3 summary identifies the May 30 decision in RF236-1 and grants $5,161, comprising $3,514 principal plus $1,647 interest. The $3,514 is the same allocation appearing in the earlier tables. This is a later disposition of the same refund proceeding, not a fresh Conlo contribution or an extra cash flow. The summary is neither the full decision/application nor bank-cleared payout proof. The $5,000 small-claims discussion concerns the refund basis, not an interest-inclusive ceiling contradicted by this award.

## Printed discrepancies retained, not silently corrected

The 1981 notice and December 1985 final decision give an audit period of April 1–September 30, **1979**. The June 1985 proposal instead prints April 1, 1979–September 30, **1980**, including its appendix. No erratum was acquired; this companion does not decide which wording was intended.

The proposal prints Queen Bee’s alleged-overcharge table value as **$4,401.97**, while the final prints **$1,401.97**; both print a **$588 potential refund**. These are source table inputs, not new findings of wrongdoing or established damages. Final table inputs sum to the stated $13,390.34 denominator; proposal inputs sum to $16,390.34. The final Queen Bee input times the printed .4194 factor equals $587.986218, consistent with rounding to $588. This numerical consistency does not authorize rewriting the proposal or claiming a formal correction. Customer addresses and the full table are intentionally omitted.

## November 13, 2026 continuation: two earlier administrative stages

### Appendix B classification, not an identified HEF account (CH2-P51)

The February 5, 1985 rule, *Petroleum Allocation and Price Regulations; Reduction of Recordkeeping Requirements*, lists **Conlo Service Inc., East Farmingdale, NY**, in Appendix B, “Firms With Completed Payments Subject to Distribution” (50 FR 4965). Section 210.1(b)(2)(i), printed p.4963, defines this class as firms which, **as of November 30, 1984**, had completed the required restitutionary payments, while those payments remained subject to DOE distribution on **February 5, 1985**. This is an affirmative agency classification, not merely an unperformed obligation.

The source gives no Conlo amount, consent-order date, HEF number, account, private signatory or registration identifier. Its correlation to the earlier HEF-0053 records rests on company name, locality, agency and compatible chronology. **The rule itself does not prove that proceeding/fund join.** It does not supply an exact payment date, independent bank verification or a finding that every liability of every Conlo corporation was extinguished. Co-listing with another company supplies no ownership, acquisition or common-fund link.

The rule describes covered records of controlled-product sales volumes and customer names/addresses, including relevant affiliate, subsidiary or predecessor records. The general retention endpoint was June 30, 1985, subject to a firm-specific extension or written release; paragraph (b) preserves duties under other applicable categories. No Conlo extension, release, compliance certificate, destruction record or present-day survival evidence was acquired. This identifies a historical records category, not evidence that those records now exist or were destroyed.

### Commitment, remittance and refund procedure are different stages

The July 25, 1979 consent, summarized in the August 13 official notice, states a **$400,000 commitment**. The notice expressly says there is **neither an admission by Tarricone nor an ERA finding of a violation**. This is an original official summary, not the acquired signed consent. Classes IV and VIII prescribe DOE checks of $66,546 and $298,808, totaling **$365,354**. Seven other classes total **$34,646**, payable through customer checks or credit memoranda. Their amounts are $14,586, $2,821, $13,213, $3,388, $61, $157 and $420. Components sum to the commitment; none is additional money. [CH2-P83](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1979-08-13/pdf/FR-1979-08-13.pdf#page=139), printed pp.47393–47394.

Exhibit 16 in the December 17, 1984 proposed refund decision, published December 26, identifies **HEF-0177** and affirmatively reports **$365,354 refunded directly to DOE**. This is agency-reported remittance, stronger than a promise but not independently bank-cleared evidence. The February 22, 1985 final decision, published March 4, repeats that same remittance and authorizes eligible applications within 90 days of publication. No claimant-specific award or executed payment instrument was acquired. The proposal and final procedure do not create two contributions. [CH2-P84](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1984-12-26/pdf/FR-1984-12-26.pdf#page=93), printed pp.50094,50101; [CH2-P85](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1985-03-04/pdf/FR-1985-03-04.pdf#page=102), printed pp.8661,8668.

The 1979 notice describes five years; both later exhibits describe the seven-class customer refund/credit commitment from August 1, 1979 through August 1, 1983. Those descriptions remain unreconciled: signed installment terms or amendments were not acquired. The proposal's ERA field says “None”; the differently labeled 1986 consent-order account field below is not silently substituted as an erratum. No alleged-overcharge amount or allocation input is treated as adjudicated damages or cash.

### Archival warning is series-specific

NC1-434-84-01's modern cover reports on **February 6, 2025** that the agency said the scheduled records were destroyed. That is a report date, not the destruction date. The schedule describes ERA/FEA petroleum-company audits conducted in 1974–1982; item 1a covers violation-determined audit case files, with destruction ten years after FARC retirement following audit/litigation completion. The audit's “permanent file” is a corporate-background folder category, not an archival permanent-retention rule. No company-specific accession, retirement or disposition record was acquired, and the consent's no-finding language does not classify its audit file. [CH2-P87](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/departments/department-of-energy/rg-0434/nc1-434-84-01_sf115.pdf), PDF pp.1–3.

The predecessor schedule's October 31, 2022 cover distinguishes **Denver FRC destruction in April 1996** from other records only **presumed destroyed**; its item 1a was superseded. Neither cover is a Tarricone/Conlo disposal certificate. [CH2-P88](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/departments/department-of-energy/rg-0434/nc1-434-81-02_sf115.pdf), PDF p.1.

OHA records have separate authorities. NC1-434-82-01 includes petitions, correspondence, orders and hearing material; its permanent proposal is crossed out and the annotated rule provides transfer to FRC and destruction at 25 years. N1-434-04-001 separately describes specified refund-process records, applications and reimbursement decisions, with disposal three years after closure or when no longer needed, whichever sooner. These authorities do not certify execution of disposal for either case, establish survival, or govern every OHA record universally today. [CH2-P89](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/departments/department-of-energy/rg-0434/nc1-434-82-01_sf115.pdf), PDF p.1; [CH2-P90](https://www.archives.gov/files/records-mgmt/rcs/schedules/departments/department-of-energy/rg-0434/n1-434-04-001_sf115.pdf), PDF p.2.

Missing evidence is a case-to-series/accession and custody/disposition crosswalk. No conclusion that all OHA, litigation, duplicate, published or payment records are gone follows. These records establish neither the ultimate recipient of money nor its subsequent use.
