# Early petroleum operating finance: a documented payment chain and its limits

Reviewed October 17, 2026. Historical companion to [CH2 claims](../../Tmanch_CH2_claim_ledger.csv) and the [earlier funding note](../../Tmanch_CH2_Early_1980s_Funding_Source_Note.md). This package adds one documentary transaction group, a financing structure, tax-payment counterevidence and a bounded corporate crosswalk. It is not a new J20 action or an aggregate of Russian-linked receipts.

## What changes

The strongest new evidence is a March 1983 fuel-purchase payment described in the May 22, 1997 Tax Appeals Tribunal decision concerning Manhattan & Queens Fuel Corp. (M&Q), DTA 810457. The decision identifies a $150,000 wire received by Intercontinental Credit Corporation (ICC) from “Chase” on behalf of Lesez, a cancelled SMI check, and an allocation in New York Fuel Terminal Corporation's (NYFT) cashbook. This moves the record beyond shared people or addresses: it documents a payment attributed to Lesez in a specific SMI inventory transaction. It does not identify the ultimate money source, personal beneficiary, or a later property-closing transfer. [CH2-P39](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/decisions/810457.2.dec.pdf), pp.13–14.

Read the [typed transaction rows](transactions.csv), [relationship rows](relationships.csv) and [source inventory](sources.md) together. A document description, invoice, accounting credit, calculated tax credit and restitution order are different evidence types. Rows within a transaction group overlap and must not be summed.

## 1. Reconstructing the March 1983 transaction

| Documentary leg | What the tribunal describes |
|---|---|
| Supplier invoice | Citgo to M&Q, March 10, 1983: 252,000 gallons, $215,712. |
| Supplier financing | An NYFT disbursements-journal entry, reference 11335, shows $215,712 due to ICC. Accountant Abbey Blatt interpreted it as an ICC wire to Citgo for NYFT. This is testimony interpreting an accounting entry. |
| Customer billing | NYFT's March 9 sales journal records the cargo for SMI; the customer invoice is $226,442. |
| Cancelled check | A copy attached to that invoice shows SMI paying NYFT $76,422. |
| Third-party wire | Another document shows ICC receiving $150,000 from “Chase” on behalf of Lesez Petroleum Corporation. |
| Allocation | NYFT's March 9 receipts journal credits $150,000 to SMI via wire. This is the accounting treatment of the same amount, not another receipt. |

The reproduced amounts reconcile only to **$226,422**, **$20 below** the printed invoice. Neither number is silently corrected. March 9 dates the receipts entry; the narrative gives no separate date for the wire document or cancelled check. “Chase” remains the printed bank label, without an inferred account holder or expanded bank identity. The original exhibits were described in the decision but were not independently acquired here. [P39, pp.13–14](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/decisions/810457.2.dec.pdf).

The transaction follows the February 21, 1983 SMI transfer recorded in CH2-BOGATIN-041. Earlier testimony also describes continuing business use after that sale; timing does not establish unchanged share ownership. This payment is an operating outflow/credit in 1983, not an additional July 1984 Lesez receipt, personal profit, or cash available for the October 1984 Tower purchase.

## 2. The factor and terminal-capital structure

The same decision says that around 1980 M&Q approached its factor ICC about financing a Citgo terminal purchase in Brooklyn. ICC referred it to affiliate Enterprise Capital Corporation. Enterprise and M&Q each held 50% of newly formed NYFT, which owned and operated the terminal. The source supplies no acquisition price, financing principal or personal owner of Enterprise. This structure supports an operating-finance explanation for ICC's role in the later payment; it does not establish knowing participation by a lender or every customer in misconduct. [P39, p.11](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/decisions/810457.2.dec.pdf).

Official NY filing metadata identifies a candidate NYFT corporation, DOS 702986, formed June 1, 1981. A formation date is not the terminal closing date and is compatible with an approximate earlier approach for financing. The decisive identity documents remain the original shareholder, financing and factoring agreements.

## 3. Tax credits prevent inflated retained-proceeds estimates

The November 1, 1991 conciliation reduction recognized SMI's payment of motor-fuel tax on **2,817,189 gallons**. At the stated eight-cent rate this implies **$225,375.12**, a calculation from recognized gallonage, not an independently inspected Treasury or bank receipt. The remaining disputed component was 10,616,779 gallons and **$849,342.32**. The printed original SMI component, $1,074,717.40, also differs by four cents from 13,433,968 gallons multiplied by $0.08; the calculation and printed figure remain distinct. Do not subtract differently scoped whole-assessment figures to derive a payment. [P39, p.6](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/decisions/810457.2.dec.pdf).

The Tribunal reversed the ALJ and sustained the assessment as modified by conciliation (pp.22–24). The earlier ALJ outcome is not the final disposition. An assessed amount does not prove collection, and neither sales revenue nor tax liability is equivalent to retained personal proceeds. This credit is positive counterevidence to treating all gallonage as unpaid-tax cash.

## 4. A documented merger, with two identity gaps still open

Official NY DOS rows place **Enterprise Capital Corp., DOS 447231**, and **Pan American Trade Development Corp., DOS 142871**, in merger filing **B262523-5**, filed August 29 and effective August 31, 1985. Enterprise is marked constituent C. Post-filing status records show Enterprise inactive and Pan American active, supporting a survivor inference; the certificate image and complete terms were not obtained. “Active” refers to that historical status, not the present. [CH2-P41](sources.md#ch2-p41--official-corporate-filing-crosswalk).

A separately preserved August 31, 1965 American Permac invoice expressly identifies its factor as **Intercontinental Credit Corporation, Division of Pan American Trade Development Corp.**, at 2 Park Avenue. This is contemporaneous business evidence in a later official DEC archive. It establishes the division style, not participation in the 1983 fuel transaction. [CH2-P42](https://extapps.dec.ny.gov/data/DecDocs/130063/Report.HW.130063.1997-03-25.FRI_Report_Appendices.pdf#page=146), physical PDF p.146.

Two gaps remain: the tax decision does not identify its Enterprise as DOS 447231, and does not identify its ICC as the Pan American division. A separately registered ICC, DOS 110143, also used 2 Park Avenue; shared name/building cannot resolve legal identity. The later Roth litigation supplies no NYFT/SMI/Lesez bridge and its judgment is excluded from these transaction totals. The 1985 merger is a genuine corporate link, not proof that every name in the operating chain belongs to the same group.

## 5. Restitution obligations are not recovered money

A prepared-statement attachment in the June 20, 1989 House hearing on Organized Crime Strike Forces lists **$5 million restitution for Michael Markowitz** and **$5 million for David Bogatin** under recoveries ordered. It supplies no itemized collection, individual docket or allocation. The attachment discusses federal and state cases. These entries cannot be added to earlier $3 million state commitments, tax assessments or each other to represent distinct money recovered. They are obligation evidence only. [CH2-P40](https://www.ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/148382NCJRS.pdf), printed pp.88,99,125/PDF p.128.

## 6. Address and professional context: useful targets, not transfers

Lesez's 1984 instructions and Diamond Terminal's July 1987 application both use **3631 Hampton Road, Oceanside**. The Diamond decision also identifies Marvin E. Kramer as counsel, independently of the already recorded Bogatin POA. This is a dated address-use/professional-service connection; neither fact supplies a transfer, common ownership, terminal succession or the provenance of purchase money. The three-year gap matters. The Diamond Tribunal expressly struck ALJ finding 53; that stricken material is excluded and cannot be restored through the earlier determination. [CH2-P43](https://www.dta.ny.gov/pdf/archive/decisions/805249.DEC.pdf), pp.1–2,16; existing CH2-P32, pp.3–4.

The street address does not identify a historical legal parcel. Later facility identifiers and modern mapping do not resolve an executed lease, landlord, title or a Crossboro/Ewe connection. No terminal-ownership edge is added. Other shared-counsel-only clients and unrelated later proceedings remain outside this package.

## Cumulative assessment and competing explanations

**Established:** a specific payment attributed to Lesez was allocated to SMI inventory; ICC participated in operating finance; Enterprise/M&Q shared NYFT ownership as described by the Tribunal; tax payments were credited; a later registry merger and an older division style are independently documented.

**Supported possibilities, not findings:** the third-party payment could reflect a commercial advance, intercompany settlement or another inventory-financing arrangement. The factoring structure makes those alternatives concrete. Without the corresponding Lesez ledger or agreement, none can be selected conclusively. Gross receipts may be owed onward to suppliers, as the existing Debbie Oil broker instructions demonstrate.

**Not established:** ultimate origin or personal ownership of the $150,000; net cash retained by Bogatin; identity of all condominium buyers; any petroleum-to-Tower closing transfer; common ownership inferred from lawyers or addresses; collected restitution totals; or Soviet/Russian direction. CH2-SOVFIN-033's insufficient-evidence conclusion remains unchanged. The new evidence strengthens the commercial payment network while narrowing what can responsibly be counted as personal proceeds.

### Highest-value missing records

1. The described wire advice, cancelled check, invoice and ICC allocation, with original headers and account titles.
2. Lesez's corresponding ledger and reimbursement/advance agreement; supplier and commission settlement records.
3. NYFT shareholder/factor agreements and merger certificate B262523-5 to resolve corporate identifiers.
4. Closing escrow statements and source debits for the specific Tower parcels.
5. Individual restitution judgments and collection/distribution records, with offsets against other orders.

These targeted records could close substantive gaps; more name-only litigation matches cannot substitute for them.
