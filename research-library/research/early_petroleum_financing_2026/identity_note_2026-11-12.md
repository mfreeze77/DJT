## November 14 supplement: plural-name Conlo bank litigation and dated record locators

### One check, three non-additive evidence stages

In *SOS Oil Corp. v Norstar Bank of Long Island*, the Court of Appeals describes a 1984 wholesale petroleum running account with **Conlo Services Corporation**, with direct deposits into SOS's Norstar account. On **July 9, 1984**, Conlo deposited a check payable to SOS with a **$255,000 face amount**, correctly reflected on the deposit slip. Norstar erroneously encoded **$25,000**, which the court says was actually debited to Conlo and credited to SOS; the deposit appeared on SOS's July 31 statement. SOS demanded the **$230,000 discrepancy** on October 31, 1985. The Court of Appeals affirmed summary judgment for SOS on **October 18, 1990**, following the December 4, 1989 appellate affirmance. [CH2-P96, Cornell-hosted judicial text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I90_0183.htm), 76 N.Y.2d 561, opening facts and conclusion.

These are **one item**, not three independent flows: face amount minus actual posting equals the judgment discrepancy. The $25,000 is an affirmative court description of actual bank posting, not just a promise. No original check, deposit slip, bank statement, independently cleared instrument or judgment-satisfaction record was acquired. The judgment is not proof of collection or a later Conlo payment. Neither the source of Conlo's funds nor SOS's subsequent use is traced. No aggregate cash figure, property payment or corporate acquisition follows.

The lower opinion describes **Conlo Services, Inc.**, gasoline running-account business, and account closure **by September 26, 1984**. It permits only one recovery for the discrepancy; alternative theories do not multiply it. The common litigation and check support a **case-level plural-name variant**, not a blanket corporate identity merge. [CH2-P97, Appellate Division text mirror](https://openjurist.org/152/ad2d/223/sos-oil-corp-v-norstar-bank-6052330), 152 A.D.2d 223,225, paragraphs 4–6,19–23.

No corporate identifier, signatory, address or registration record here joins those plural names to **Conlo Service Corp.**, **Conlo Service, Inc.**, or **Conlo, Inc.** in the earlier DTA/DOE or other records. The Court of Appeals says the account had been closed for more than a year by the October 1985 demand and the business had ceased; account closure and that description are not a dissolution certificate. No dormant-license, successor or subsequent acquisition explanation is invented. The decision concerns bank liability, not a general adjudication of the underlying business. Footnote 1 says Norstar's separate third-party action against Conlo was not before the court; its disposition was not acquired.

### Historical registration sources: locator guidance, not a transaction finding

The following are historical departmental descriptions, not current legal advice, acquired session laws or proof that any Conlo filed a particular form. They refine what contemporaneous records could be relevant. No existing repository backdating error is established by this supplement.

- **TSB-M-85(1)M, July 17, 1985:** new applicants submit **TP128.3**, an unqualified current financial statement certified within the preceding 18 months by a CPA pursuant to an audit, and estimated monthly gallonage without excluding exempt sales. Existing registrants could be required to provide **TP187.16** and audited financial information on review. The Commission could waive the financial-statement requirement and require a bond instead, so the rule does not prove a specific audit exists. No contemporary blank form or completed Conlo filing was acquired. [CH2-P98](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/fuel_%26_gasoline/m85_1m.pdf), pp.1–3.
- **1985 change notice:** TSB-M-86(1)M describes section 283(5)'s written address/officer/director/partner and residence-address change notices as effective **June 1, 1985**. Its downloaded PDF has a later historical-purpose cover, so printed p.2 is physical p.3. The contemporaneous TSB-M-85(5)M corroborates the change-notice categories in its tax-enforcement discussion. These record targets do not themselves identify an owner or seller. [CH2-P100](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/misc/m86_1m.pdf), printed p.2; [CH2-P99](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/fuel_%26_gasoline/m85_5m.pdf), pp.1,5.
- **1986 voting-share transfer regime:** TSB-M-86(9)M dates Chapter 276 changes to **July 14, 1986**. Its added section 283(9) includes partner changes and an acquisition giving another person more than 10% of voting shares, with section 283(10) substituting **25% or more where there are four or fewer voting shareholders**. It describes transaction/date/party/interest/responsible-person information and a 75-day determination period after a properly complete application and requested additional information. Lack of prior written approval is grounds for cancellation/suspension, not proof that cancellation occurred. A limited inheritance exception uses a revised application within 90 days. Do not project this added framework backward onto December 1985. [CH2-P101](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/memos/fuel_%26_gasoline/m86_9m.pdf), pp.1,6–7.

New York Article 12-A registration is distinct from federal IRS Form 637 registration. Neither a federal registration narrative nor these state memos establishes an actual state transfer application, execution of an acquisition, bond payment or the identity of a particular Conlo. Filled filings and executed transfer instruments remain missing; non-location is not proof they never existed.
