## A completed corporate acquisition, not a purchase of the lessor

LUKOIL's 2002 consolidated accounts report acquiring **72% of Getty Petroleum Marketing Inc. for $53 million in December 2000**, then the **remaining 28% for $20 million in January 2001**. The rounded components imply approximately **$73 million for the same acquisition**. The total is a derived summary, not a third payment or verified shareholder allocation. [SEC original, Note 18, printed p.101 / PDF p.103](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/vprr/0303/03032473.pdf#page=103), CH2-P116; observations EP-CGL01–EP-CGL03.

Separate lessor **Getty Realty** confirms the December 8 tender and January 25 merger dates. It describes its 1997 spin-off of Marketing, continued ownership/leasing of station and terminal properties, and a fifteen-year initial master lease effective December 9, 2000. Buying Marketing equity did not itself convey Getty Realty's fee ownership. [Getty Realty 2001 first-quarter 10-Q, Note 2 and General](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000095013701501554/c62504e10-q.txt), CH2-P117; EP-CGL04–EP-CGL05.

## The 2001 dispute concerned how stations were operated

At the **April 2, 2001 Judiciary hearing**, Brent Coscia identified himself as Consumers Petroleum's sales director and opposed S.B.1426. Getty president/COO Vincent DeLaurentis described 92 Connecticut stations supplied, including 74 commission arrangements: dealers ran locations, while Getty set gasoline prices. He said Getty purchased from domestic refiners, not its foreign parent. Getty and a supporting operator argued that the arrangements supported supply availability and reduced operators' inventory-capital burden. These are attributed operating statements and competing policy arguments, not inspected contracts or measured consumer outcomes. [Official hearing](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2001/juddata/chr/2001JUD00402-R001300-CHR.htm), CH2-P118; EP-CGL06–EP-CGL08.

The [introduced bill](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2001/tob/s/2001SB-01426-R00-SB.htm) was broader than preservation of the existing 74 arrangements. It proposed an exception for qualifying producer/refiner acquisitions on or after January 1, 2001 of distributors already operating in Connecticut on January 1 through commission-based independent contractors. It would allow them to **continue to open and operate** stations, repeal §14-344b, and take effect July 1. Those are proposed terms, not enacted permissions. The December tender and January merger straddled the proposed cutoff; the sources do not resolve how the proposed rule would have applied to that two-stage acquisition. CH2-P119; EP-CGL09.

The [official bill history](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/asp/cgabillstatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB01426&which_year=2001) lists March 26 referral and March 29 scheduling of the April 2 hearing, but no favorable report, passage or act number. The [2025 codification](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2025/pub/chap_250a.htm) retains §14-344b and no proposed acquisition exception; §§14-344a/b cite only P.A.79-437. The [May 2, 2006 OLR report](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2006/rpt/2006-R-0321.htm) also treats both restrictions as operative. Together these support **not enacted as introduced**, without inventing a defeat vote or Getty-specific enforcement outcome. CH2-P120–CH2-P122; EP-CGL10–EP-CGL12.

The law addresses producer/refiner **station operation**, including specified employee, subsidiary, commission-agent and fee-contractor arrangements, with limited exceptions. That is not a blanket prohibition on wholesale supply or property ownership. OLR's jobber explanation is not a court ruling applying the statute to a particular company.

## The licensed distribution channel remained distinct

DRS **AN 2002(1), issued February 25, 2002**, lists licensed status **as of January 1, 2002**. It separately names **GETTY PETROLEUM MARKETING INC** and **GETTY TERMINALS CORP** in each of three categories:

- Diesel Fuel Distributors, tax type 50;
- Motor Vehicle Fuels Distributors, tax type 52;
- Gasohol Distributors, tax type 57.

These are two named entity snapshots across three categories, not six businesses or transactions. This supports a licensed motor-fuel tax-distribution channel after the legislative proposal, not actual sales, retail-operation permission, Russian-origin fuel, property ownership or common ownership inferred from the shared listed address. The announcement is now expressly obsolete and retained for reference. [Original DRS list, headings, Getty entries and issuance line](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/publications/announcements/2002/an-2002-1), CH2-P114; EP-CGL13–EP-CGL14.

## Commercial benefit and boundaries

The completed equity acquisition gave Russian corporate parent LUKOIL ownership exposure to an existing U.S. marketing/distribution network and, on Getty's testimony, retail-price-setting arrangements. This is a concrete **corporate commercial benefit**, not demonstrated Russian-state revenue or political direction. Getty and the operator described domestic-supply and small-operator advantages, but quantities, savings and realized effects remain unverified. No benefit is attributed to enactment of S.B.1426.

The separate **2022 Consumers asset acquisition** by Global Partners is not this transaction. Its [January 31, 2022 8-K, Item 2.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1323468/000110465922009711/tm224849d1_8k.htm) names a five-entity seller group and January 25 completion. The short 2001 testimony name does not alone identify every later seller. CH2-P111 is a comparison source only; no additional monetary observation is added.

Existing **CH2-P75/P76 and EP-CEN18–20,27–28** concern the distinct 2006 Bridgeport property/business and issuer-accounting record. The [Bridgeport seller-identity extension](century_bridgeport_note_2026-11-14.md#november-14-seller-identity-extension), **EP-CEN30–38**, documents the Consumers/CPW facility and management overlaps; it supplies no tie to Getty equity, seller accounts or ownership. **EP-CEN37 / CH2-P111** already records the separate 2022 retail transaction. This package does not identify Connecticut Petroleum Wholesalers, Consumers Petroleum and Getty as one company. It does not connect the acquisition to the 1983 Lesez/SMI transaction (**CH2-P39, CH2-FIN-056–058, EP-G01, EP-T04–06**), Trump property payments or RF-003/027/028 aggregates. [J20-041](../../Tmanch_CH7_J20_event_ledger.csv) is later LUKOIL licensing context, and [CH7-LIG-004](../../Tmanch_CH7_claim_ledger.csv) records separate divestment-performance limits. This sidecar cannot establish a corporate-perimeter or authorization link from the U.S. Getty entities to LUKOIL International GmbH or GL 128/130.

## November 14 Getty operating and settlement follow-through

### One operator's reported response, not a statewide outcome

In [Connecticut’s February 15, 2005 hearing](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2005/gldata/chr/2005GL-00215-R001000-CHR.htm), Wilton Getty owner Mike Lindquist said that after LUKOIL's purchase of Getty Marketing, the state's separation of retail station operation from producer/refiner control let him move away from a 5.5-cent-per-gallon commission arrangement and earn a gasoline profit without relying on his repair business. **CH2-P127 / EP-CGL31** is a retrospective, attributed observation about one station, not an audited margin, verified regulator decision, station ownership/title conclusion or industry-wide result. It supplies no exact conversion date or replacement contract. The hearing concerned separate H.B.6653, not the 2001 S.B.1426 disposition. Read alongside the 2001 competing testimony (**EP-CGL06–08**) and the limited tax-distribution records (**EP-CGL13–14**).

### Estate receipt and downstream creditor recoveries

Keep the earlier acquisition and Realty's distinct fee-ownership/Marketing's leasehold (**EP-CGL01–05**) separate from the later Marketing estate. Realty's [FY2011 Form 10-K, History of Our Company, p.7](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000119312512117565/d285705d10k.htm) says LUKOIL transferred its ownership interest in Marketing to Cambridge Petroleum Holding Inc. on **February 28, 2011** and that Marketing filed for Chapter 11 on **December 5, 2011**. This is an issuer-reported exit before bankruptcy, not an executed sale instrument, proof of Cambridge's beneficial owners, or a transaction valuation. Do not treat former LUKOIL ownership as continuing at bankruptcy or attribute the subsequent estate's cash position to the former owner on that basis. **EP-CGL32.** Realty's [FY2015 Form 10-K, Note 3, pp.54–55](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000119312516500301/d62672d10k.htm) separately reports that an October 2012 arrangement authorized up to **USD 6.725 million** of Realty funding; Realty reports **USD 6.526 million** actually advanced in aggregate. Those are ceiling and deployment, not additive flows.

Realty's contemporaneous [July 17, 2013 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000115752313003422/a50674938ex99-1.htm) described a then-conditional **USD 93 million** collective payment to the estate by or on behalf of the defendant group. The 10-K reports approval on July 29 and estate receipt in August 2013. It separately identifies **USD 25.096 million** distributed to Realty under the funding agreement and **USD 6.585 million** distributed on postpetition priority claims. The funding distribution includes **USD 7.976 million** advance repayment plus interest, **USD 13.994 million** rent/real-estate tax recovery and **USD 3.126 million** partial damages; these sum to USD 25.096 million, not incremental withdrawals. Realty's **USD 34.251 million** 2013 annual receipts from the estate overlap the distributions; the observed disclosure does not allocate a residual beyond the stated categories. The earlier release's estimated Realty recovery is not a separate realized amount. No bank instrument or defendant-by-defendant contribution is shown. **EP-CGL15–23.**

Under the [March 3, 2015 signed agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000119312515083549/d889258dex991.htm), the trustee and three Realty companies agreed to **USD 170 million in allowed unsecured claim face value**, not cash. The terms contemplated a catch-up distribution and **USD 550,000** in disputed funding proceeds subject to final court approval; releases have specified parties and exceptions. Realty's 2015 filing separately reports approval and actual April 22 distributions of **USD 6.8 million** on unsecured claims and **USD 550,000** on the funding dispute, followed by approximately **USD 10.8 million** in final distribution November 3. The filing's **USD 18.177 million** annual amount is not added to, or forced to match, the partly rounded distributions. Neither the filing nor executed agreement supplies independently bank-cleared amounts. The agreement recites August 24, 2012 confirmation; the 10-K summarizes plan approval as July 2012. Preserve the difference without treating either as a payment date or resolving it from missing docket material. **EP-CGL24–28.**

### Separate 2020 settlement and lease assignment control

The [FY2020 Form 10-K, Note 3, printed p.59](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000156459021008446/gty-10k_20201231.htm) describes settlement and dismissal of a separate New York remediation-cost recovery case against LUKOIL Americas and certain then-current or former executives. The settlement was effective **December 17, 2020** and the reported payment to Realty was **USD 20.5 million net of contingency fees**. This is a reported realized payment under settlement, not independently dated bank clearance, a gross payment amount, an adjudicated finding, or a known own-account transfer by any named defendant or insurer. The purported case theories are not adopted. The same filing's unrelated New Jersey consent order and reported USD 13.5 million Realty outflow are not this settlement. **EP-CGL29.** Another FY2015 disclosure identifies June lease assignments to Global subsidiaries from White Oak and Big Apple; it does not establish the assigned properties had all previously been leased to Marketing. **EP-CGL30.**

### Cumulative significance and beneficiaries

This advances the record from ownership and operating access to actual **issuer-reported estate and domestic creditor recoveries**. The estate and Realty benefited through paid distributions, which include return of advances, taxes/rent and some damages rather than pure profit. Settling defendants obtained litigation resolution. One American operator reports a commercial benefit from adapting his contract; the impact beyond that station is uncertain. The **net national beneficiary remains uncertain**: no Russian-state revenue, whole-company lifetime net result, exclusive LUKOIL funding, or Trump link is established. Beneficiary categories cannot be collapsed across the original share purchase, gross estate payment, downstream receipts, allocation components, annual totals, claim face value and separate net settlement.

No new amount enters central or Trump-linked aggregates. Missing records include payment instruments, defendant and insurer allocations, bankruptcy ledger and complete costs, the 2020 gross-to-net and original release, plus the exact station contract. None alters the 2006/2022 Global/Consumers distinctions (**EP-CEN18–29, EP-CEN35–38**) or later license outcomes (**J20-041 / CH7-LIG-004**).
