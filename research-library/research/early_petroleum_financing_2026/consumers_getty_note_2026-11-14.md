# Consumers, Getty and LUKOIL: acquisition, operating model and licensing distinctions

[Source provenance](consumers_getty_source_manifest_2026-11-14.json) distinguishes original records, testimony, proposed legislation and interpretation. ## A completed corporate acquisition, not a purchase of the lessor

LUKOIL's 2002 consolidated accounts report acquiring **72% of Getty Petroleum Marketing Inc. for $53 million in December 2000**, then the **remaining 28% for $20 million in January 2001**. The rounded components imply approximately **$73 million for the same acquisition**. The total is a derived summary, not a third payment or verified shareholder allocation. [SEC original, Note 18, printed p.101 / PDF p.103](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/vprr/0303/03032473.pdf#page=103), CH2-P116; observations EP-CGL01–EP-CGL03.

Separate lessor **Getty Realty** confirms the December 8 tender and January 25 merger dates. It describes its 1997 spin-off of Marketing, continued ownership/leasing of station and terminal properties, and a fifteen-year initial master lease effective December 9, 2000. Buying Marketing equity did not itself convey Getty Realty's fee ownership. [Getty Realty 2001 first-quarter 10-Q, Note 2 and General](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1052752/000095013701501554/c62504e10-q.txt), CH2-P117; EP-CGL04–EP-CGL05.

## The 2001 dispute concerned how stations were operated

At the **April 2, 2001 Judiciary hearing**, Brent Coscia identified himself as Consumers Petroleum's sales director and opposed S.B.1426. Getty president/COO Vincent DeLaurentis described 92 Connecticut stations supplied, including 74 commission arrangements: dealers ran locations, while Getty set gasoline prices. He said Getty purchased from domestic refiners, not its foreign parent. Getty and a supporting operator argued that the arrangements supported supply availability and reduced operators' inventory-capital burden. These are attributed operating statements and competing policy arguments, not inspected contracts or measured consumer outcomes. [Official hearing](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2001/juddata/chr/2001JUD00402-R001300-CHR.htm), CH2-P118; EP-CGL06–EP-CGL08.

The [introduced bill](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2001/tob/s/2001SB-01426-R00-SB.htm) was broader than preservation of the existing 74 arrangements. It proposed an exception for qualifying producer/refiner acquisitions on or after January 1, 2001 of distributors already operating in Connecticut on January 1 through commission-based independent contractors. It would allow them to **continue to open and operate** stations, repeal §14-344b, and take effect July 1. Those are proposed terms, not enacted permissions. The December tender and January merger straddled the proposed cutoff; the sources do not resolve how the proposed rule would have applied to that two-stage acquisition. CH2-P119; EP-CGL09.

The [official bill history](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/asp/cgabillstatus/cgabillstatus.asp?selBillType=Bill&bill_num=SB01426&which_year=2001) lists March 26 referral and March 29 scheduling of the April 2 hearing, but no favorable report, passage or act number. The [2025 codification](https://prdext3.cga.ct.gov/2025/pub/chap_250a.htm) retains §14-344b and no proposed acquisition exception; §§14-344a/b cite only P.A.79-437. The [May 2, 2006 OLR report](https://prdext2.cga.ct.gov/2006/rpt/2006-R-0321.htm) also treats both restrictions as operative. Together these support **not enacted as introduced**, without inventing a defeat vote or Getty-specific enforcement outcome. CH2-P120–CH2-P122; EP-CGL10–EP-CGL12.

The law addresses producer/refiner **station operation**, including specified employee, subsidiary, commission-agent and fee-contractor arrangements, with limited exceptions. That is not a blanket prohibition on wholesale supply or property ownership. OLR's jobber explanation is not a court ruling applying the statute to a particular company.

## The licensed distribution channel remained distinct

DRS **AN 2002(1), issued February 25, 2002**, lists licensed status **as of January 1, 2002**. It separately names **GETTY PETROLEUM MARKETING INC** and **GETTY TERMINALS CORP** in each of three categories:

- Diesel Fuel Distributors, tax type 50;
- Motor Vehicle Fuels Distributors, tax type 52;
- Gasohol Distributors, tax type 57.

These are two named entity snapshots across three categories, not six businesses or transactions. This supports a licensed motor-fuel tax-distribution channel after the legislative proposal, not actual sales, retail-operation permission, Russian-origin fuel, property ownership or common ownership inferred from the shared listed address. The announcement is now expressly obsolete and retained for reference. [Original DRS list, headings, Getty entries and issuance line](https://portal.ct.gov/drs/publications/announcements/2002/an-2002-1), CH2-P114; EP-CGL13–EP-CGL14.

## Commercial benefit and boundaries

The completed equity acquisition gave Russian corporate parent LUKOIL ownership exposure to an existing U.S. marketing/distribution network and, on Getty's testimony, retail-price-setting arrangements. This is a concrete **corporate commercial benefit**, not demonstrated Russian-state revenue or political direction. Getty and the operator described domestic-supply and small-operator advantages, but quantities, savings and realized effects remain unverified. No benefit is attributed to enactment of S.B.1426.

The separate **2022 Consumers asset acquisition** by Global Partners is not this transaction. Its [January 31, 2022 8-K, Item 2.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1323468/000110465922009711/tm224849d1_8k.htm) names a five-entity seller group and January 25 completion. The short 2001 testimony name does not alone identify every later seller. CH2-P111 is a comparison source only; no additional monetary observation is added.

Existing **CH2-P75/P76 and EP-CEN18–20,27–28** concern the distinct 2006 Bridgeport property/business and issuer-accounting record. The [Bridgeport seller-identity extension](century_bridgeport_note_2026-11-14.md#november-14-seller-identity-extension), **EP-CEN30–38**, documents the Consumers/CPW facility and management overlaps; it supplies no tie to Getty equity, seller accounts or ownership. **EP-CEN37 / CH2-P111** already records the separate 2022 retail transaction. This package does not identify Connecticut Petroleum Wholesalers, Consumers Petroleum and Getty as one company. It does not connect the acquisition to the 1983 Lesez/SMI transaction (**CH2-P39, CH2-FIN-056–058, EP-G01, EP-T04–06**), Trump property payments or RF-003/027/028 aggregates. [J20-041](../../Tmanch_CH7_J20_event_ledger.csv) is later LUKOIL licensing context, and [CH7-LIG-004](../../Tmanch_CH7_claim_ledger.csv) records separate divestment-performance limits. This sidecar cannot establish a corporate-perimeter or authorization link from the U.S. Getty entities to LUKOIL International GmbH or GL 128/130.

## Data and source controls

The 16-column CSV contains 14 observations. All aggregation, amount-counting, transaction-counting and bank-clearance flags are false. EP-CGL01/EP-CGL02 are components of one acquisition; EP-CGL03 summarizes them and must never be added. Empty amounts mean nonmonetary or unquantified, not zero. DRS category repetition is not a new money event.

Original-source hashes and acquisition limits are in the manifest. Main-host CGA failures were resolved using official mirrors. The SEC acquisition page was visually inspected; HTML/text records were read. Original archives are not proposed publication files. Missing records include acquisition settlement/shareholder allocation, station contracts, actual sales/price data, Getty-specific operating treatment and precise Consumers corporate continuity. No wrongdoing, illicit-financing or Russian-origin-supply inference is added.
