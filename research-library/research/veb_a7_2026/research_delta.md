## 1. Most consequential evidence obtained

### A. The steel seller can now be tied to original Toronto security documents

The July 23, 2010 judgment **Luxe Holding Ltd v Midland Resources Holding Ltd, [2010] EWHC 1908 (Ch), HC10C01784**, identifies Guernsey **Midland Resources Holding Limited (MRHL)** as seller. Paragraphs 22–24 describe negotiations through Troika Dialog, a May 19 sale agreement with the **“Troika buyers”**, a **USD850 million** price, and completion accepted by the claimant by May 26. The buyer SPVs remain unnamed. This is an interim-injunction judgment, not a final trial determination of every assertion. It does **not** identify VEB, establish the buyer-financing facility, or mention Toronto. (S17, paragraphs 5–6, 22–27.)

The earlier May 4 agreement with **Luxe** was a different USD690 million transaction. The judgment records a USD50 million initial payment, and a later MRHL letter concerning its return plus a separate USD50 million contractual payment. Paragraph 16 identifies **MRHL director Derek Roe** and refers to SWIFT confirmations enclosed with the letter. Those confirmations and the actual closing statement were **not obtained**. Paragraph 45 identifies audited 2009 accounts, March 2010 management accounts and later cash evidence; its discussion combines asset disposals and debt repayments. It must not be turned into a clean USD850 million seller-account credit. (S17, paragraphs 9, 14, 16, 45.)

The 1,029-page Ontario application record supplies something beyond the Receiver's security-document list:

- **Exhibit V**, physical pages 631 and 649–650, contains the October 9, 2007 **cost-overrun, completion-guarantee and standstill agreement**, with separate signed counterparts for MRHL and BNY Trust Company of Canada as agent. The MRHL signature block names **Derek Roe, Director**. This is a concrete name-and-role overlap between the steel seller's paperwork and Toronto financing obligations; it is **not** an A7 overlap. Signatures were visually read, not forensically authenticated.
- **Exhibit W**, physical pages 653–655, reproduces registered charge **AT1599260**, October 9, 2007: Talon as chargor, MRHL as chargee, **CAD200 million face principal**, 15% interest and associated guarantee terms. This is the registered instrument copy, not merely its inclusion in a list. The face value is not an advance.
- Jay Wolf's affidavit, paragraphs 69–74, reports **CAD105,651,246 including accrued interest**, plus **CAD9,315,000 guarantee fees**, as of September 30, 2016, based on advice from Midland counsel. These are reported debt amounts, not a verified cash ledger. The guarantee fee belongs to the Midland arrangement, **not Trump**. Paragraph 74 reports assignment of the debt and charge to **Midland Development Inc. (MDI) on/about June 6, 2011**; it says the assignment was not registered on title. The executed assignment and its consideration remain missing. (S18.)

**What this changes:** MRHL is no longer only a broad Shnaider/Midland label in the Toronto investigation. We have a named seller, an exhibited Toronto guarantee and charge, a matching director name, and a precisely dated reported intercompany debt assignment. These make tracing specific and materially strengthen the commercial connection **within the Toronto/steel history**. They still do not identify which dollars funded the guarantee, any alleged USD15 million contribution, or a Trump fee.

## 4. Cross-period comparison and competing explanations

**Strengthened:** MRHL's role on both sides of the steel/Toronto inquiry; Derek Roe's director-role match within those records; historical VEB/Shor co-ownership of Exim; A7/PSB ownership; additional A7-named financial instruments and a described PSB/A71 channel.

**Still institutional, not transactional:** VEB/VEB.RF is an identifiable institution, but its original steel buyer-financing file is still absent. No obtained document supplies an account, facility number, approving official, signatory or payment intermediary linking that transaction to A7/Exim. A7's 2024 formation excludes it as the same legal actor in 2010; it does not eliminate the possibility of later reuse of institutions or people, which must be tested separately.

The evidence supports a meaningful question about recurring Russian state-financial involvement, while leaving the Toronto provenance allegation unresolved. An ordinary explanation—separate state-bank activities and commercial MRHL project obligations financed from fungible assets—fits the records obtained. A more specific influence explanation would require matching credit approvals, intermediaries, instructions or payments. A signed political instruction is not the only possible evidence, but the documents here do not establish knowledge of Russian origination by Trump, dependence on VEB, coordination with A7, or control by Russia.

The adjacent Garantex/Peresvet/Rosneft, Exved/Deutsche Bank, Baku/IRGC, Abramovich and Kushner/Gorkov material remains separate. Existing qualifications and denials are preserved. No new transaction bridge was found in the scoped material reviewed; these leads were not exhaustively reinvestigated and are not added to the core graph merely to enlarge it.
