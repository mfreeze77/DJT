### 1. The court order itself recognizes MDI as assignee, within a defined legal context

The **December 20, 2016 Amending Order**, in CV-16-11573-00CL, was acquired as a 105-page PDF. The signed order, physical page 2 paragraph 2, restates the appointment order with retroactive effect to November 1, 2016. Schedule A paragraph 30, physical page 17 / printed 14, identifies Midland Development Inc. as MRHL's assignee when establishing priority for the administration and receiver-borrowing charges. This is court-order text, not just the earlier affidavit or counsel repeating it. (S37; M31.)

**Strengthened:** MDI was recognized in the receivership priority framework as the assignee of the MRHL interest. **Not resolved:** the executed June 2011 deed, consideration, original advances, principal/interest allocation or source of funds. The order does not adjudicate a steel-proceeds transfer or authenticate the underlying debt ledger. Its temporal boundary is the receivership, not a contemporaneous 2011 bank event. Only selected order pages and navigation thumbnails were inspected; the 105-page scan was not exhaustively read.

The user's chronology qualification is retained: the 2007 guarantee predates the 2010 steel sale. A later sale could supply liquidity for an existing obligation, or unrelated assets could do so. A matching director of the same company is useful for locating records but is not an independent indication of coordination. Neither fact chooses between those explanations.

### 2. Receiver accounting is not a substitute for project operating accounts

The **Third Receiver's Report**, March 8, 2017, expressly says the receiver did not possess or control the respondents' operations or cash flow and instead received periodic financial reporting from named respondents. Its terms of reference disclose unaudited inputs. Physical page 6 has an inconsistent 2017 start year; paragraph 7(a) describes the period beginning December 13, 2016. The inconsistency is preserved, not silently repaired. (S38; M32.)

The **Final Receiver's Report**, August 23, 2019, gives an additional exclusion: paragraph 22, physical page 8, says consideration for the hotel and residence assets took the form of a **credit bid**, leaving no cash sale proceeds for distribution from those transactions. Its separate cash statement covers purchaser funding, the Waterous sale, interest, taxes, secured-lender distributions and professional costs. Paragraph 18 identifies receiver, counsel and CBRE fees; these are not Trump licensing fees. (S39; M33.)

**Consequence:** a clean final receiver account cannot establish either that earlier Trump fees were paid or that they were absent. A transaction price, credit setoff, respondent cash report and receiver cash statement must be reconciled separately. This qualifies the usefulness of the archive rather than discarding it.

### 4. A7 publishes the custody procedure and the documents that could connect note serials to holders

A7's legal page links historical and current **standard custody terms**. Actual bytes were obtained for the August 2025, December 2025 and April 2026 versions, with distinct hashes. The August version identifies A7 by **OGRN 1247700586891 / INN 9710137165** in section 10. It specifies a certificate register recording successive custody-right holders (section 3.2.4), and a chain-of-rights check when a certificate is lost (section 5.2). Section 5.3 links a release request to a payment instruction. These are standard contractual procedures, **not an executed Aragon agreement or an obtained issuer register**. (S41–S44; M35–M38.)

The August version's Appendix 4, physical page 10, requests the note serial, denomination, beneficiary-bank details and ruble conversion for payment. Thus a USD face amount is not itself evidence of a USD bank transfer. The April version contains additional notification/electronic-document provisions; these must not be retroactively applied to 2025 holdings. Both the historical and current section 9.3 qualify how changes affect existing contracts. (S42; S44.)

**New acquisition target:** match each of the 13 existing serials to its actual note copy, purchase/accession documents, custody certificate, register entry, transfer instruction/acceptance and redemption instruction, then reconcile any bank settlement. A holder change may involve transfer of custody rights, not a new bank wire at that moment. That is a conditional investigation mechanism, not proof any identified holder used it.

**Unchanged:** Aragon's table does not supply the issuer's OGRN. The published A7 terms identify their issuer, but no obtained record connects an Aragon serial to that issuer's register. N22 remains an unresolved candidate distinct from N07. No VEB origination, foreign beneficiary or wrongdoing is inferred.
