# Separate policy appendix: Russian diesel and ICC sanctions

**Cutoff: October 9, 2026. No rows from this appendix are added to the transaction ledger.** Source keys resolve in `sources.csv`. Legal-instrument effects are distinguished from actual payments and inferred motives.

## Russian diesel: an authorization, not a completed purchase

OFAC **General License135, dated October9,2026**, authorizes transactions otherwise prohibited by the Russia-related regulations in 31CFR587 and589 that relate to sale, delivery, offloading or importation—including into the United States—of Russian-origin diesel. The stated endpoint is **12:01a.m. eastern daylight time, April7,2027**. Paragraph(b) does not authorize debiting accounts on a U.S. financial institution's books belonging to Russia's Central Bank, National Wealth Fund or Finance Ministry. (S30, complete one-page instrument, paragraphs a-b; page image inspected.)

This is a **general licence**, not a newly enacted statute, a purchase order, an appropriation, a government procurement contract or proof of deliveries. The instrument does not establish quantities bought, buyers, prices, invoices, settlement banks, Russian fiscal receipts or an A7 payment channel. No underlying claim of a separately enacted diesel statute was authenticated in this wave.

**Economic inference, not transaction tracing:** completed purchases could generate revenue for Russian sellers and potentially support state resources. Gross sales receipts are not the same as additional usable state revenue: costs, taxes, ownership, alternative buyers, discounts, restrictions and substitution matter. State resources can support military capabilities without each incoming dollar being earmarked. Nevertheless, these instruments do not trace a diesel payment to Iranian assistance or a particular attack on Americans. Neither zero economic effect nor a specific attack-financing chain is established here.

Most decisive next records: actual customs entries and bills of lading; named commercial buyers and sellers; contracts and settled invoices; lawful payment confirmations; and, for any claim of U.S. government procurement, the relevant agency award and payment records. No purchases, outreach or record requests were sent.

## ICC: institution designated, but exceptions materially qualify the effects

OFAC's **October9,2026 action lists the ICC entity itself** under ICC-EO14203. This is more than the pre-existing targeting of particular individuals. The underlying executive order is **EO14203 of February6,2025**. Its stated rationale concerns U.S. and allied personnel and the ICC's actions; the government's characterization is a stated rationale, not independent adjudication of jurisdiction or all motives. (S31; S34.)

The same action includes exceptions that prevent an accurate account from stopping at “the institution was sanctioned”:

- **GL13:** authorizes specified maintenance or wind-down activity through **12:01a.m. EDT April7,2027**, including enumerated routine activities and transactions involving ICC blocked accounts for those purposes. It is not merely an instruction to cease all activity immediately. (S32, paragraphs a-c.)
- **GL14:** authorizes specified telecommunications, internet communication services and enterprise-software activity, including use of ICC blocked accounts for authorized transactions. It preserves limitations involving other blocked persons and other federal requirements. **No April2027 expiry is stated in this instrument.** Do not import GL13's deadline into GL14. (S33, paragraphs a-e and notes1-2; both pages read.)

The issuance page also links GL15 andGL16. Their full contents were not used to reach the conclusions here. This appendix does not claim every relevant exception or operational consequence has been exhaustively reviewed.

**Effects versus motive:** sanctions and compliance uncertainty may burden an international accountability institution even when exceptions permit activity. Potential benefit to Russia or other subjects of ICC scrutiny is an effects hypothesis; it is not proof Russia caused the measures, dictated their terms, or exchanged support for them. Actual vendor decisions, payment refusals, staffing consequences and case effects must be examined rather than assuming a universal immediate technology or banking cutoff. The licenses are significant counterevidence to that blanket characterization, not proof of no burden.

No obtained record connects the ICC action to the A7 pledge, a VEB facility, Toronto financing or diesel settlement. These remain separate analyses.
