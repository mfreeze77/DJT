# Bushehr: contract, reported advance and sanctions scope

This note supplements the [existing Iran-support source note](Tmanch_CH7_Oct12_Iran_Statement_Source_Note.md#documented-russian-engagement-and-practical-support). ## Commercial relationship and reported payment

A [Rosatom release republished by NO RAO on March 15, 2017](https://www.norao.ru/press/news/1488/) identifies Iran's Nuclear Power Production and Development Company (NPPD) as customer and JSC ASE, within Rosatom's engineering division, as general contractor. It describes their November 2014 contract as EPC turnkey construction of Bushehr Units 2 and 3. This is an institutional description, not an inspected executed contract or financial annex. (BUS-S01)

[Rosatom's 2016 annual report](https://report.rosatom.ru/go/rosatom/go_rosatom_2016/go_rosatom_2016.pdf), section 3.3.2, printed page 92 / PDF page 43, says the first advance from the Iranian side was received in **December 2016**. A [November 7, 2017 sector account](https://strana-rosatom.ru/2017/11/07/busher-2-simvol-treh-pokolenij/) reports an advance **transfer on December 28, 2016**. The month-level receipt and later-reported transfer date are different fields; an exact receipt date is not established. (BUS-S02/S03)

These sources document a **corporate-reported payment event**, not an independently inspected bank receipt. Neither identifies the amount, currency, account, bank, intermediary or transaction reference. The contractual customer and contractor must not be substituted for verified payment-specific remitter and beneficiary names. The amount remains unknown/null, not zero and not the total project estimate. The first advance is not total payments over the contract's life.

The November 2017 account describes Units 2/3 as fully Iranian-financed at that time. That dated description cannot establish the financing structure in later years. It also reports subsequent local subcontracts, without values or payment evidence. (BUS-S03)

## Amount and project controls

- The [August 26, 2016 Rosatom Newsletter](https://rosatomnewsletter.com/2016/08/26/new-reactor-units-for-iran/) contains both $10 billion and $11 billion project estimates. These are not separate transactions or verified paid amounts. Its separate €2.2 billion proposed Russian loan-package claim is attributed to Bloomberg; no Bushehr-specific allocation or executed loan is supplied. (BUS-S04)
- An [IRNA interview dated September 12, 2021](https://en.irna.ir/news/84467902/Russia-s-allots-5b-loan-to-Iran-to-develop-Bushehr-Ramin-power) attributes a $5 billion Russian loan-allocation claim to parliamentarian Mojtaba Yousefi. It names Bushehr and Ramin, uses imprecise geography, and does not identify Units 2/3, an executed credit agreement, project allocation or drawdown. This is an **unverified financing lead**, not proof of Russian credit to the two units or money paid to their contractor. (BUS-S05)
- Gabriel Noronha's [April 17, 2024 House testimony](https://docs.house.gov/meetings/FA/FA13/20240417/117148/HHRG-118-FA13-Wstate-NoronhaG-20240417.pdf), page 8 and footnote xxxv on page 15, describes $10 billion per reactor. Its [cited March 16, 2022 article](https://freebeacon.com/national-security/new-iran-agreement-would-let-russia-cash-in-on-10-billion-contract-to-build-nuclear-sites/) instead describes $10 billion for two additional units. Do not multiply that figure or equate either account with paid revenue. Official hosting does not make externally authored testimony a government finding. (BUS-S06/S07)
- [Rosatom's September 24, 2025 release](https://atommedia.online/en/press-releases/sostoyalas-vstrecha-glavy-rosatoma-alekseya-likhacheva-s-vitse-prezidentom-irana-prezidentom-organiz/) concerns an Iranian SMR memorandum, not the 2014 Bushehr EPC payment. [IRNA's September 26, 2025 report](https://www.irna.ir/news/85949922/) concerns a separate four-unit Sirik/Hormozgan agreement; its valuation is not independently verified here. Neither announcement establishes a Bushehr 2/3 disbursement. (BUS-S08/S09)

No USD/EUR equivalent, estimate, loan allocation, debt balance or later repayment claim is added to a paid total. Unit 1 fuel/operations accounts must be reconciled separately from Units 2/3 construction accounts.

## Dated sanctions scope, not a payment authorization finding

| Record | Supported scope | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| State, May 3, 2019; policy effective May 4 | Assistance expanding Bushehr beyond its existing reactor could become sanctionable; existing-unit support had distinct treatment | A statement of historical scope, not a current transaction determination |
| State, May 27, 2020 | A 90-day extension for international support to Unit 1; same-day briefing specified operations/safety, fuel supply and take-back, excluding additional units | The July 27 wind-down deadline for separately terminated projects must not be labeled Bushehr's deadline |
| Blinken's written answers in the March 22, 2023 hearing record | Unit 1 operations, training and services included; additional Bushehr construction expressly excluded | March 22 is the hearing date, not a separately established written-answer date; underlying waiver and current validity are not established here |
| OFAC GL 115D, effective June 11, 2026 | Specified EO 14024 transactions involving listed entities, solely maintaining/supporting civil-nuclear projects initiated before November 21, 2024, through 12:01 a.m. EST December 18, 2026 | Bushehr is not named; restrictions and exclusions remain; not proof of Iran-program authorization or use of any listed bank |

Primary sources: [2019 State legal-practice digest, p. 509](https://2017-2021.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/2019-Digest-Chapter-16.pdf); [May 27, 2020 State statement](https://2017-2021.state.gov/keeping-the-world-safe-from-irans-nuclear-program/); [same-day briefing](https://2017-2021.state.gov/briefing-with-special-representative-for-iran-and-senior-advisor-to-the-secretary-brian-hook-and-assistant-secretary-for-international-security-and-nonproliferation-dr-christopher-a-ford-o/); [2023 official hearing record](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-118shrg52967/html/CHRG-118shrg52967.htm); [GL 115D](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/935701/download?inline=). (BUS-S10–S14)

[ITSR §560.101](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-31/subtitle-B/chapter-V/part-560/subpart-A/section-560.101) separates Iran prohibitions from licenses issued under other sanctions provisions. GL 115D operates under EO 14024 and 31 CFR part 587, not as a blanket Iran authorization. Historical State waivers concerning foreign participation and OFAC permissions for transactions within U.S. jurisdiction are not interchangeable. (BUS-S15)

**Current-document gap:** No 2025/2026 Bushehr-specific State waiver instrument, renewal chain, expiry date or revocation notice was retrieved. The 2023 answer is not carried forward by assumption. No located payment-specific OFAC license identifies a Bushehr payer, beneficiary, amount or bank. These are bounded search limits, not findings that every activity is permitted or prohibited.

## Records needed to quantify the financial relationship

1. Executed NPPD–ASE contract, price, payment schedule and amendments.
2. Invoice, advance guarantee and remittance/receipt for the reported December 2016 advance, with exact dates, entities, amount, currency and banks.
3. Any project-specific credit agreement and drawdowns, separating Bushehr from other infrastructure and Unit 1 from Units 2/3.
4. Reconciled construction and fuel/operations accounts, dated later payments and remaining balances.
5. Current Iran-specific waiver instruments and any transaction-specific licenses applicable to an identified payment.

The finding is a documented commercial relationship and an unquantified corporate-reported advance. It does not establish a quantified financial benefit, settlement route, individual receipt or transaction-specific sanctions conclusion.
