# October 15 Meeks bill: introduction and proposed Russian-energy restrictions

Reviewed October 17, 2026. Supports **J20-2026-027** in [J20-Pres.md](J20-Pres.md) and the [structured ledger](Tmanch_CH7_J20_event_ledger.csv). This records congressional introduction and a published proposal, not enacted sanctions or a license cancellation. No screening score is assigned.

## Introduction, announcement and provenance

The [October 15 Congressional Record, H6045, PDF page 3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-10-15/pdf/CREC-2026-10-15.pdf#page=3), records Gregory Meeks introducing **H.R. 10828**, concerning Russian oil and gas extraction and refinement sanctions, with 14 original cosponsors. It refers the bill to Foreign Affairs and five additional committees: Judiciary, Financial Services, Ways and Means, Oversight and Government Reform, and Rules.

The [House Foreign Affairs Committee minority's October 16 release](https://democrats-foreignaffairs.house.gov/2026/10/meeks-introduces-legislation-to-block-trump-s-russia-diesel-deal) identifies the proposal as the **Prohibit Corrupt Russia Oil Deals Act** and lists 17 cosponsors, including Ted Lieu, Steny Hoyer and Don Bacon, who are absent from the introduction list. The lists are dated snapshots, not 17 verified original cosponsors. The Record controls the introduction date despite the later release's same-day framing. The bill title is the sponsor's label; no misconduct finding is made here.

The [12-page distributed draft](https://mcusercontent.com/e711646c72c197262ff8d3d32/files/7e271885-d73a-21d1-b105-fa189d823911/MEEKS_158_xml.pdf) was retrieved through [the Kyiv Independent's direct bill link](https://kyivindependent.com/us-lawmaker-introduces-bill-blocking-trumps-russian-diesel-deal-2/) after the official release's redirect failed. All page-labelled text was inspected. It carries an October 15, 10:26 a.m. drafting stamp, a blank bill number and blank referral field. Its short title matches the release and long title matches the Record, but this review does not certify identity with the filed H.R. 10828 text. The sections below describe that distributed draft.

## Proposed scope, conditional on enactment

- **Section 2, p. 2:** would prohibit DFC support or participation in arrangements concerning energy owned by a Russian foreign person, including assets abroad.
- **Section 3, pp. 2–3:** would nullify GLs 133, 134A, 134B, 134C and 135, and restrict future licensing/authorization of otherwise-prohibited Russian oil/gas transactions. Only subsections 3(a)–(b) sunset three years after enactment.
- **Section 4, pp. 4–6:** would expand CAATSA review to EO 14024-emergency orders and specified energy relief. The energy-review period has a separate endpoint: consulted State certification of war termination and credible commitment to a just peace including Ukrainian compensation.
- **Section 5, pp. 6–10:** would require asset-blocking and immigration sanctions within 30 days of enactment on Russian oil/gas extraction, refining/production and maritime-transport participants. The admission exception concerns international obligations; humanitarian and authorized U.S. intelligence, law-enforcement and national-security exceptions apply to section 5, not as blanket exemptions from sections 2–4.
- **Sections 6–7, pp. 10–12:** would require license-impact reports and reports investigating specified energy-company involvement in abuses against Ukrainian civilians. Both start 30 days after enactment; subsequent intervals are 60 days for three years and 180 days for two years, respectively. Reporting requirements are not findings.

Introduction precedes [J20-2026-025's October 16 Graham Act waiver order](Tmanch_CH7_Oct16_Graham_Waivers_Source_Note.md). It must not be described as a response to that later order or as restoring the Graham Act's tariff provisions. A proposed nullification also does not establish that every listed older license remains operative.

**Analytical inference:** The proposal is countervailing congressional pressure that could constrain Russian energy financing and strengthen Ukrainian/allied leverage if enacted and implemented. Introduction alone establishes neither foregone Russian revenue, lower consumer prices, DFC investment, completed diesel purchases nor changed executive policy. Legislative prospects, implementation and economic results remain unestablished.
