# November 10 EPA diesel renewals effective November 14

**J20-2026-105/106 · CH7-EPA-DIESEL-003/004.** Lee Zeldin signed distinct Texas and Arctic Alaska Clean Air Act waiver letters on **November 10, 2026**. Both are effective **November 14 through December 3, 2026** (20 days inclusive). They succeed, rather than recount, the October 20 [Texas and Alaska instruments](Tmanch_CH7_Oct20_EPA_Diesel_Waivers_Source_Note.md), **J20-2026-051/052 · CH7-EPA-DIESEL-001/002**, whose 20-day windows ran October 25–November 13. The new federal legal window is verified; qualifying fuel use and results are not.

## Operative scope and conditions

- **EPA-DIESEL-S004, Texas:** [Signed letter](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-11/fuel-waiver-txled-ulsd-2026-1110.pdf), pp.1,3–4. Waives the TXLED State Implementation Plan requirements and the specified federal diesel-sulfur requirements under 40 C.F.R. § 1090.305 for qualifying use in Texas. Distribution and sale ceiling is **up to 500 ppm sulfur** (p.4); other passages describe eligible older engines originally certified for fuel below 500 ppm. EPA describes eligibility by **pre-2007 highway engine model year** and **pre-Tier-4 nonroad engines**, not blanket vehicle model-year permission; EPA notes certification labels and category-specific timing. The letter says manufacturers should report qualifying production per batch under § 1090.935(a)(1)(iv), marked as emergency-waiver fuel.
- **EPA-DIESEL-S005, Arctic Alaska:** [Signed letter](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-11/fuel-waiver-alaska-arctic-2026-1110.pdf), pp.1,3–5. Temporarily waives the specified federal diesel-sulfur requirements under § 1090.305 for originally eligible engines **fueled and operated** in Alaska north of the **66th parallel** (p.1 n.1, the letter's definition here). The distribution/sale ceiling is **up to 5,000 ppm sulfur**. EPA describes **pre-1994 highway engine model years** and **pre-Tier-3 nonroad engines**, subject to original certification/category. Local topping-refinery production is the stated supply mechanism, not measured output. Apart from warning-bearing product transfer documentation, the letter waives other Part 1090 diesel-sulfur reporting requirements; it does not replicate Texas batch reporting.
- **Both:** The letters record DOE concurrence and EPA's supply/public-interest determinations under Clean Air Act § 211(c)(4)(C), attributing disruption in part to Middle East issues. Non-ULSD must be segregated from product sold or labeled as ULSD; engine use with particulate filters or catalytic emission controls is excluded. Required anti-misfueling warnings substitute for the specified designation requirement. Unrelated applicable requirements are not waived. Qualifying fuel introduced into domestic pipelines or certified at a manufacture/import facility before expiry, documented by product transfer documents or certificates of analysis, may be sold/distributed in the subject area until that identified volume is depleted. Thus December 3 ends this issuance window, not all authorized qualifying sell-through. The certification provision is not permission to import a prohibited cargo.
- **EPA-DIESEL-S006:** [EPA index](https://www.epa.gov/gasoline-standards/fuel-waivers) lists both November 10 actions as renewals and preserves the October 20 predecessors. This is publication corroboration from the issuing agency, not an independent performance dataset. [Acquisition provenance](Tmanch_CH7_Nov10_EPA_Diesel_Renewals_Source_Manifest.json) records three retained originals; operative pages and signatures were visually inspected.

## Beneficiary assessment and existing-history joins

**United States/allies through domestic supply flexibility, with a conditional domestic environmental downside; outcomes unmeasured.** The direct legal benefit accrues to eligible U.S. suppliers and users: allowing compatible older engines to draw on separately handled higher-sulfur fuel can ease demand on constrained 15-ppm fuel; the Arctic letter identifies an additional local supply route. Relief for modern engines or wider allied resilience would be indirect and depends on actual substitution. Higher-sulfur use can carry a pollution and maintenance tradeoff; no realized volumes, price effects, emissions, fiscal savings, delivery or proceeds are established. The two geography-specific instruments are separate legal acts in the same policy track, not independent economic-outcome observations.

This is affirmative evidence of continuing domestic mitigation, not just a possible explanation.
