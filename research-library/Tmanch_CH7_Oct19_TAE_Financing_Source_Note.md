# TMTG–TAE financing: corporate funding and unresolved contract-summary differences

Reviewed October 19, 2026. Companions: [typed observations](research/tae_financing_2026/transactions.csv) and [source manifest](research/tae_financing_2026/source_manifest.json). This is source-controlled corporate context, with no new J20 event, score, or addition to historical financial totals.

## Parties and cash direction

Trump Media & Technology Group Corp. (TMTG), a Florida corporation, is the holder/lender; TAE Technologies, Inc., a Delaware corporation, is the issuer/borrower. The December 18, 2025 merger agreement also includes TMTG's wholly owned T Media Sub, Inc. The contemplated merger would leave TAE as TMTG's subsidiary. [TAE-S09, Item 1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036125046056/ef20061577_8k.htm).

TAE's audited Note 9 reports **$200 million funded December 19, 2025**. [TAE-S04, F-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031534/djt20260922_s4.htm#notes_annual). TMTG's 2025 cash-flow statement records the corresponding $200 million convertible-note investment outflow. Those are two observations of the same advance. Aggregate corporate cash flows do not identify the particular wire's ultimate capital sources. [TAE-S05, cash-flow statement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036126007174/ef20062365_10k.htm).

The September 30 8-K describes **up to $100 million additional draw capacity**, with the window ending November 29, 2026 or earlier merger-agreement termination. Capacity does not establish a draw. [TAE-S03, Item 1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/djt20260930_8k.htm). The records reviewed at the October 19 checkpoint did not establish a later second-tranche payment. The dated October 29 supplement below now records later issuer funding disclosure while preserving that acquisition history.

## September 30 voting rights and interest

The amended unsecured note grants pre-conversion voting units calculated as:

`floor(0.25 × (outstanding principal + unpaid accrued interest) / Original Issuance Price of the then-most senior equity security)`

Series 12's reference price is $50. Using the reported $200 million principal and $10,969,863.01 interest once gives **1,054,849 illustrative units**. The factor is not 25% ownership or a verified percentage of voting power. Rights apply to combined common/preferred votes and quorum, with separate-class and Requisite Holder votes excluded. [TAE-S02, §§3(c), 3(i)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/ex_1020868.htm).

## Discrepancies to preserve

- **Financing trigger:** The September 30 8-K describes automatic conversion on qualifying financing. [TAE-S03, Item 1.01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/djt20260930_8k.htm). Both signed notes expressly exclude financing itself as a conversion/prepayment trigger. The earlier clause instead links later conversion to the financing's securities and price. [TAE-S01, §3(a)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036125046056/ef20061577_ex10-1.htm); [TAE-S02, §§3(a), 3(i)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/ex_1020868.htm). TAE's audited note also contains automatic-conversion summary language. [TAE-S04, F-15](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031534/djt20260922_s4.htm#notes_annual). No legal resolution or actual conversion is inferred.
- **Termination timing:** The 8-K compresses the termination branches. The signed notes specify conversion immediately before scheduled maturity, with the mandatory/elective branch depending on the termination reason. [TAE-S03](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/djt20260930_8k.htm); [TAE-S01, §§3(c)–(e)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036125046056/ef20061577_ex10-1.htm).
- **Maturity date:** The S-4 says December 19, 2027 on pp.119 and 133, but December 18, 2027 in audited Note 9's F-16 table. Neither date is silently selected. [TAE-S04](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031534/djt20260922_s4.htm#notes_annual).
- **PIK presentation:** The original note permits interest on capitalized interest. [TAE-S01, §1(b)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036125046056/ef20061577_ex10-1.htm). The amended preamble bars capitalization while §1(b) retains principal-increase wording; it expressly bars interest-on-interest. Its $10,969,863.01 unpaid interest balance matches simple 7% accrual for 286 days from December 18. This arithmetic does not resolve presentation or the earlier accrual treatment. [TAE-S02, preamble and §1(b)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000143774926031535/ex_1020868.htm).

### Investor rights and intermediary roles

Issued common shares carry shareholder voting rights. Unissued conversion shares in this table do not establish exercisable common-stock votes; the indenture also permits cash or mixed conversion settlement. Noteholders have creditor and contractual-consent rights, distinct from board control. [TAE-S24, p.27]; [TAE-S21, §§5.1–5.3].

The filed note-subscription form's §8(i) collective amendment route requires the Company and majority principal **including each qualifying holder group at $25 million or more**. The grouping includes affiliates and controlled/managed funds or accounts; the threshold applies to commitments before closing and holdings afterward. Individual consent protections cover specified adverse/disproportionate changes, purchase-price changes and amendments to specified sections, including §6. The provision also protects placement agents for specified adverse changes. This is not merely a majority-holder test, and the historical conversion-share list does not establish who currently qualifies. These are filed-form terms; no executed waiver, present veto holder or covenant breach is inferred. [TAE-S20, §8(i)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1849635/000114036125020420/ef20049689_ex10-2.htm).

The closing release identifies Yorkville Securities and Clear Street as co-lead placement agents, with BTIG and Cohen & Company Capital Markets as co-placement agents. The note form identifies the latter as a division of **J.V.B. Financial Group, LLC**. Cantor Fitzgerald & Co. is described as financial adviser in the release and included among defined placement agents in the form; both source-specific descriptions are retained. Clear Street also has a separately disclosed security allocation. No investment is inferred solely from an advisory role.
