# October 22 Fitzpatrick bill: authenticated introduction, energy-license limits and Ukraine intelligence support

**J20-2026-048 / CH7-REAGAN-BILL-001.** The [authenticated introduced H.R. 10866](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119hr10866ih/pdf/BILLS-119hr10866ih.pdf), pp. 1–4, and the [October 22 Congressional Record, H6055, PDF p. 3](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CREC-2026-10-22/pdf/CREC-2026-10-22.pdf#page=3), establish introduction by Brian Fitzpatrick on **October 22, 2026**, with Marie Gluesenkamp Perez and Don Bacon, and referral to Ways and Means and additionally Foreign Affairs and Intelligence (Permanent Select). The [October 23 sponsor release](https://fitzpatrick.house.gov/2026/10/fitzpatrick-introduces-ronald-reagan-peace-through-strength-act-to-block-russian-oil-imports-and-defend-ukrain) is a later announcement despite its same-day wording. The bill and Record control the introduction date.

The original four-page introduced bill was acquired and read in full. It supersedes an acquisition boundary, not earlier legislative history: the sponsor's [distributed draft](https://fitzpatrick.house.gov/index.cfm?File_id=8CC6DF45-BEBB-4C22-9E35-B6C42D40B111&a=Files.Serve) has an October 21 drafting stamp and blank number/referral fields. The numbered GovInfo introduced version is now available, so the following scope is grounded in that version rather than inferred from a press summary.

## Proposed scope, not enacted law

- **Section 2, p. 2:** would amend the Ending Importation of Russian Oil Act to bar OFAC waivers/general licenses for importing the covered Russian energy products and prohibit Treasury expenditure for an agreement violating that Act.
- **Section 3, pp. 2–3:** would nullify GL 135 and bar Treasury authorization of otherwise-prohibited transactions ordinarily incident and necessary to Russian crude-oil or petroleum-product sale, delivery or offloading. The future-transaction clause is not confined to the narrower import-language summary.
- **Section 4, pp. 3–4:** would require the DNI to ensure relevant intelligence-community elements provide Ukraine authorized information, intelligence and imagery support specifically intended or reasonably expected to defend or retake Ukrainian territory. Each element's head would have to notify the congressional intelligence committees within 24 hours after an interruption begins. That is **notification after interruption**, not a prior-approval requirement or a universal guarantee that interruptions cannot occur. The text does not itself regulate every private satellite-service contract or compel delivery of Patriot interceptors.

No passage, enactment, actual GL 135 cancellation, implemented intelligence safeguard, discharge petition or foregone Russian revenue is established by the records inspected.

## What this adds to the cumulative record

This is a concrete legislative response, not just another expression of opposition. It links two already documented policy mechanisms in one introduced instrument: Russian-energy market access and the continuity of Ukraine's intelligence support. The proposed change would constrain executive licensing discretion and make interruptions more visible to congressional overseers. That is relevant to the project's control-after-warning test: lawmakers are proposing additional constraints because existing arrangements have not prevented the recorded conflict over relief and support.
