# Graham Act enactment: source and implementation boundaries

## Source and scope note, reviewed October 12, 2026

The White House signing notice and GPO public-law text independently identify September 18 as enactment/signature date. The statute is the controlling source for scope. The first 30-day date is October 18, not yet reached at this review.

- Section 102(a) requires review and sanctions/blocked-vessel identifications for persons and vessels determined to meet its criteria within 30 days and every 180 days thereafter. This is not proof that all contemplated designations have occurred.
- Section 109 prohibits U.S.-person purchases of Russian sovereign debt upon enactment; therefore the entire act should not be described as merely future authority.
- Sections 112–113 set 30-day tariff requirements, capped at 500% for Russian goods and 100% for qualifying third-country goods. They do not automatically establish those maximum rates as collected tariffs. Section 113 includes country/purchase predicates, an exception, and reporting requirements. Section 113(g) requires rate/methodology justification to specified congressional committees at least ten days before imposing the covered duties; an absent public report does not establish non-submission.
- Section 114(h)(1) protects U.S. persons operating under pre-enactment Treasury general licenses; section 114(h)(2) expressly preserves Treasury authority to extend or issue new licenses. Section 115 permits waivers subject to a national-interest certification and explanatory report to Congress. Exceptions and discretion must be retained in any account of the act.
- Section 201 extends the Iran Sanctions Act sunset from 2026 to 2031. It is not proof of newly executed Iran sanctions.
- Section 114(h) does not, by itself, resolve whether GL135 complies with the separate 2022 Public Law 117-109 import prohibition. This note makes no finding on the senators' diesel-legality allegation and supplies no adjudication or waiver record.

## Cumulative relevance and attribution

This is material legislative counterevidence to a claim that the U.S. pressure architecture only weakened. Attribute the enacted framework to Congress plus Trump's signature, and assess executive determinations, licenses, waivers, collections and effects separately. Potential Ukrainian/allied benefit is greater statutory pressure on Russian financing and evasion; actual revenue denial, trade displacement and enforcement effects remain unestablished by enactment alone. No new benefit/cost score or motive finding is proposed.

## October 15 follow-up: section 113 product definitions and evasion scope

[Public Law 119-111, section 113(g)(2)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-119publ111/html/PLAW-119publ111.htm) expressly applies its HS2709 crude-oil and HS2711 natural-gas definitions to the importer test in subsection (c)(1). Subsection (c)(2) separately covers the top five countries facilitating Russian oil sanctions evasion, with the conduct defined in subsection (i)(2). The crude-oil definition alone therefore does not establish a blanket diesel exemption from the act.

This is a clarification of the statutory scope supporting J20-2026-012, not evidence of new implementation, collected duties or a finding that the reported Morocco-bound diesel cargo triggers duties.

## October 15 House letter: implementation questions and license-revocation request

[McCaul's official release and reproduced letter](https://mccaul.house.gov/media-center/press-releases/mccaul-costa-colleagues-urge-full-and-faithful-implementation-lindsey-o), dated October 15 and inspected October 16, identifies Michael McCaul, Jim Costa and 20 colleagues: 22 House members. They urged Graham Act implementation and GL 135 revocation, requested answers before October 18 and a committee briefing afterward, and sought trade/maritime-data methodology and benchmarks for Russian energy-revenue and third-country purchase reductions.

This is a distinct House request, not the [October 13 twelve-senator letter](Tmanch_CH7_Oct13_Intelligence_Diesel_Source_Note.md). It supplements [J20-2026-011, -012 and -015](J20-Pres.md) without adding an event or score. Congressional questions establish neither revocation, completed implementation, legal invalidity, executive response nor realized revenue effects. The official page supplies a reproduced text and named signers; this review did not independently authenticate signature images.

**Analytical inference:** The requested reporting could make executive implementation and revenue-pressure claims more testable, potentially supporting Ukrainian/allied leverage. Compliance and outcomes remain unverified. Preserve the statutory exceptions and licensing/waiver distinctions above.
