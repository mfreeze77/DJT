## Presidential remarks and source limitations

The [Roll Call / Factba.se transcript](https://rollcall.com/factbase/trump/transcript/donald-trump-press-gaggle-before-marine-one-departure-october-13-2026/) is titled "Press Gaggle: Donald Trump Speaks to Reporters Before Marine One Departure - October 13, 2026." Its rendered transcript and embedded 10:11 video player were inspected in the cloud browser.

- **00:59–01:16:** Asked whether intelligence sharing with Ukraine had stopped, Trump denied that it had and affirmed continued sharing while describing efforts to end the war. This verifies his public assurance, not the contents, timeliness or full operational scope of intelligence delivered.
- **01:55–02:22:** Asked about concerns that diesel proceeds could fund Russian attacks, Trump said he had not been told anything and characterized the arrangement as supplying the world market rather than the United States, citing high world prices and refinery damage. At **08:01–08:20**, he again described a world-market arrangement and claimed lower prices. The claimed price effect is attributed to Trump, not established by this record.
- [Pool report 9632](https://pool.pp.tools/reports/9632), attributed to New York Times White House correspondent Shawn McCreesh, records the intelligence exchange. It says the report was sent October 13 at 12:20 p.m.; the independent archive displays 16:27 UTC. The reporter expressly asks readers to check quotations against audio. This review inspected the transcript and pool text, but did not independently audit the recording's audio.

The intelligence assurance is relevant counterevidence to a current blanket-cutoff claim. It does not retroactively negate the distinct March 2025 suspension and restoration recorded in [J20-014](https://github.com/mfreeze77/DJT/blob/382563338d6906d51d506891df6bb9cd6dc638a4/J20-Pres.md#L86), or establish that every intelligence channel was unrestricted.

## Controlling license versus statements about destination

[OFAC General License 135](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937216/download?inline=), dated **October 9, 2026**, authorizes covered Russian-origin diesel sale, delivery, offloading and importation transactions under 31 CFR parts 587 and 589 through **12:01 a.m. EDT April 7, 2027**. Paragraph (a) expressly includes importation into the United States. Paragraph (b) excludes debits to U.S.-financial-institution accounts of Russia's Central Bank, National Wealth Fund or Ministry of Finance. It contains no already-loaded cargo cutoff.

The [official issuance notice](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261009_33) and [recent-actions index](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions) were inspected October 13. The displayed index contained no later GL 135 amendment or rescission. The published October 9 document was retrieved October 13: 190,208 bytes; SHA-256 `b42bed871cff3162540bc645eba298e4c0cf73d0a5b1712879a3f4fec060d65b`. Trump's world-market explanation does not amend its written scope. Permission to conduct covered transactions is not proof that U.S. importation, government procurement, payment or any particular delivery occurred.

The separate proposed license event is [J20-2026-011 at PR30's preserved head](https://github.com/mfreeze77/DJT/blob/3aa4b02cf2bb6d3872b657526e05a70c77ff5e2e/J20-Pres.md#L133). This note does not alter that entry or the license matrix.

## Russian officials' stated fiscal mechanism and negotiating position

**Anton Siluanov:** [Interfax, October 13 at 15:33 Moscow / 12:33 UTC](https://www.interfax.ru/business/1122124), places the finance minister's remarks to journalists in the State Duma. He described expected company export revenue, foreign currency and balance-of-payments support. Asked about additional budget revenue, he said there was no diesel export duty; improved company financial results would yield tax payments. [Vesti's direct interview report, displayed October 13 at 15:26](https://www.vesti.ru/ns/v-minfine-rf-obyasnili-kak-dokhody-ot-sdelki-po-dizelyu-otrazyatsya-na-byudzhete), corroborates the tax mechanism. Its [separate interview report](https://www.vesti.ru/ns/siluanov-ehksport-dizelya-podderzhit-platezhnyj-balans-rossii) addresses revenue and balance-of-payments support.

This is an attributed forecast of a fiscal channel: company revenue and improved financial results leading to taxes. It supplies no amount of additional revenue, tax receipts, delivered cargo or realized benefit. The sources are direct reporting of his remarks, not obtained fiscal accounts or a ministry-hosted transcript.

**Sergei Ryabkov:** [Interfax, October 13 at 17:32 Moscow / 14:32 UTC](https://www.interfax.ru/russia/1122156), reports its own question to the deputy foreign minister in Moscow. Ryabkov rejected the premise that Russia had made Ukraine-settlement concessions in exchange for the diesel arrangement. He said Russia favored a political solution but would pursue its goals militarily if the other side was unwilling. This verifies his denial and stated position, not the actual absence of concessions or undisclosed terms. His relative reference to a presidential call two days earlier is not used to date that call.

These Russian statements are not established as responses to Trump's later gaggle or to the Senate letter. They do not show a secret bargain, personal payment, common financial channel or presidential knowledge of undisclosed terms.

## October 15 follow-up: IEA market forecast

The [IEA's October 2026 Oil Market Report](https://www.iea.org/reports/oil-market-report-october-2026), published October 14 and inspected October 15, forecasts that easing Russian diesel export restrictions is unlikely to materially change the global gasoil balance because operational Russian refinery capacity is the limiting factor. Its gasoil/diesel section projects a seasonal surplus in 1Q27, but says replenishment of accumulated inventory losses beyond normal seasonal movements is unlikely before 2H27.

This forecast qualifies the supply-and-price rationale attached to [J20-2026-014](https://github.com/mfreeze77/DJT/blob/96aa28cdfee48cc44a5d25dbde1fdc44b2286043/J20-Pres.md#L136), alongside the authorization recorded in [J20-2026-011](https://github.com/mfreeze77/DJT/blob/96aa28cdfee48cc44a5d25dbde1fdc44b2286043/J20-Pres.md#L133). The report does not name GL 135 or isolate its effects. It establishes neither zero price or trade effects from that license nor realized Russian revenue. It does not amend the license. This is follow-up evidence for existing events, not a new action or duplicate event.

**Analytical inference:** The assessment weakens expectations of a large near-term consumer-supply benefit while leaving the legal commercial opportunity for Russian sellers intact; realized gains remain unmeasured.

It does not contradict the license’s express authorization of qualifying U.S. imports, prove procurement by the U.S. government, or establish a realized Russian revenue or consumer-price benefit. It is follow-up evidence for existing events, not a duplicate event.

## October 16 follow-up: SPR crude-exchange solicitation

DOE's [FY27 SPR Oil Release No. 1 RFP](https://www.spr.doe.gov/posting/EXCHANGE/FY27%20SPR%20Oil%20Release%20No.%201/1-Request%20for%20Proposal/FY27%20SPR%20Oil%20Release%20No.%201%20Request%20for%20Proposal.pdf), **DE-RP96-27PO00001**, is dated October 16, 2026. Its cover letter requests bids by **October 21 at 11:00 a.m. Central Time** for exchange of up to **17 million barrels of sour crude**. Printed B-1 allocates Bryan Mound deliveries of 1 million barrels in November and 6 million in December 2026, and Big Hill deliveries of 3 million in December and 7 million in January 2027. Returns are scheduled in 2028–29; minimum premiums vary from 12.5% to 14%, with additional return-period premiums. The tender does not commit DOE to award contracts. B-1/B-2 warn that earlier tenders have priority and throughput is not guaranteed. The cover letter and allocation table were inspected October 16; the table was visually checked.

**Allocation and implementation limits:** DOE's [September 29 announcement](https://www.energy.gov/articles/united-states-energy-department-continues-execution-strategic-reserve-release-commitments) placed its earlier 40-million-barrel offer within the previously announced 172-million-barrel U.S. commitment. [Reuters' October 16 report, republished here](https://squawknews.com/news/511054-update-1-us-solicits-exchange-of-up-to-17-million-barrels-of-oil-from-strategic-reserv), likewise places the new offer within that commitment. These figures must not be summed as separate realized supply. DOE's [current tender listing](https://www.spr.doe.gov/posting/exchange/current) showed no FY27 award, and its [archive listing](https://www.spr.doe.gov/posting/exchange/archive) showed no award document for FY26 Release No. 4 when inspected October 16. The precise overlap with September's allocation remains unresolved; an absent posted award does not prove no award occurred. No new delivery is established. DOE's [October 16 inventory table](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/opr/spr-quick-facts) reports 282.2 million barrels in stock, not immediately available delivery capacity.

**Product and beneficiary mechanism:** [EIA](https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=327&t=8) explains that a 42-gallon crude barrel typically yields about 11–13 gallons of distillate, alongside other products, with variable yields. Crude therefore is not a one-for-one substitute for refined diesel. Potential beneficiaries are participating refiners/traders through temporary feedstock access and consumers through possible supply/price relief; awards, throughput, refining and transport condition those effects. Future premium returns depend on contract performance. The RFP also describes historically blended inventory, including Urals; this is not evidence of a new payment to Russia.

**Analytical connection:** This supplies context for the refinery-restraint request (**J20-2026-007**), diesel authorization (**J20-2026-011**), presidential price rationale (**J20-2026-014**), Senate challenge (**J20-2026-015**) and Ukrainian revenue forecast (**J20-2026-024**) in the [event ledger](J20-Pres.md). It documents another U.S. supply-response instrument, without establishing that GL 135 was necessary or fully avoidable, or measuring Russian revenue or consumer savings. It adds no event or numerical score.
