# Source Integrity Audit
## Active synthesis files and citation controls
### Updated August 26, 2026

## Purpose

This audit responds to a serious defect in earlier synthesis material: several draft-era sections cited placeholder URLs containing labels such as `fictional_reference` or `(fictional)`, while other entries linked only to a publisher homepage rather than the article or record supporting the claim.

Those links are unacceptable in an evidence project. A source that cannot be traced to the claimed record cannot carry evidentiary weight, even when the underlying event later proves true.

## Files corrected in this tranche

- `Current_Working_Tmanch_Conclusion.md` — completely replaced from the Chapters 1–7 evidence architecture.
- `DJT_TLDR.md` — completely replaced with a current, source-controlled executive synthesis.
- `J20-Pres.md` — corrected in the Chapter 7 tranche before this audit; fictional and generic links were removed and every promoted event was reconciled.

## Active source-controlled research architecture

The authoritative research path is now:

1. `Tmanch_CH1.md` through `Tmanch_CH7.md`.
2. Each chapter’s support file, claim ledger, primary-source inventory, counterevidence file, and open-questions file.
3. `J20-Pres.md` and the Chapter 7 reconciliation, event ledger, OFAC matrix, payoff ledger, and Russian-language appendices.
4. `Current_Working_Tmanch_Conclusion.md` and `DJT_TLDR.md` as synthesis products derived from those controlled files.

Earlier large narrative files remain useful as research history and lead collections, but they are not the controlling citation layer when they conflict with the chapter packages.

## Citation rules

### 1. No fictional or placeholder links

Prohibited examples include:

- URLs containing `fictional_reference`;
- URLs containing invented event-page slugs marked `(fictional)`;
- links generated as illustrative stand-ins rather than real records;
- citations to nonexistent government pages.

If an event is real but the cited URL is false, the citation must be replaced with an authentic record before the event is promoted.

### 2. No generic publisher homepages as proof

A bare link to Reuters, Politico, AP, Lawfare, TASS, RIA, or another homepage does not substantiate a claim. Citations must point to:

- the exact article;
- the official document;
- the court docket or opinion;
- the specific agency action;
- the archived page;
- or a source inventory entry identifying the unavailable record and its limitations.

### 3. Primary records first

Preferred sources include:

- court pleadings, judgments, transcripts, and exhibits;
- deeds, mortgages, corporate filings, and SEC records;
- congressional reports and testimony;
- Treasury, OFAC, DOJ, FBI, ODNI, State, DOD, White House, NATO, U.N., and foreign-government records;
- original StB and FBI records;
- contracts, letters, emails, calendars, visitor logs, and financial records when public.

High-quality documentary reporting is used when the primary record is unavailable, classified, sealed, foreign, or not publicly released. The text must identify that limitation.

### 4. Separate event, effect, motive, and control

A record may prove that an action occurred without proving why it occurred. Every major conclusion should distinguish:

1. the event;
2. its immediate effect;
3. who objectively benefited;
4. the decision-maker’s stated motive;
5. the likely influence mechanism;
6. whether direction, coercion, purchase, or control is established.

### 5. Attribute counterevidence correctly

An action does not rebut a Trump-centered influence hypothesis merely because it occurred during a Trump administration.

The synthesis must identify whether the action was:

- initiated and sustained by Trump;
- mandated by Congress;
- driven by Cabinet, military, intelligence, or career officials;
- financed and implemented by allies;
- adopted after public or institutional backlash;
- announced but never implemented;
- implemented temporarily and later reversed;
- or materially diluted by waivers and general licenses.

The controlling attribution file is `Tmanch_Conclusion_Attribution_Matrix.csv`.

### 6. Russian-language material has defined limits

Russian official, state-media, commercial-media, Telegram, and message-board records may establish:

- public Russian demands;
- state-approved framing;
- perceived gains;
- elite and nationalist expectations;
- narrative diffusion;
- later disappointment.

Anonymous message-board posts do not prove secret agreements, intelligence tasking, or American motive. They are reception evidence and lead generators.

## Remaining legacy-cleanup program

The following older files should be treated as historical working papers until they receive the same source-by-source modernization:

- `DJT 2015-2021.md`
- `DJT 2021-Pres.md`
- `Tmanch.md`
- any older narrative section that cites another repository chapter rather than the underlying record

Their factual leads remain valuable. Their citations do not supersede the controlled chapter packages.

Future cleanup should classify every external link as:

- verified and live;
- verified through an archived copy;
- replaced by a primary source;
- demoted to a lead;
- removed as unsupported;
- or retained with an explicit access limitation.

## Non-negotiable standard

No conclusion in this repository should become stronger merely because an unsupported link sounds plausible. The conclusion may be forceful only where the cumulative record is forceful.

Conversely, absence of a signed confession or recruitment file does not erase a circumstantial case built from independently sourced access, motive, vulnerability, concealment, assistance, institutional weakening, repeated beneficiary alignment, and later official action.
