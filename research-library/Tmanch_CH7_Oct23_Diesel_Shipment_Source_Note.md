# October 23 diesel departure statement and reported U.S.-bound cargo

**J20-2026-040** records the October 23 announcement/reporting; the reported loading date is October 22. No additional benefit score or monetary flow is booked.

[PRIME's original October 23 report](https://1prime.ru/20261023/peskov-874554171.html), timestamped 12:45 Moscow (09:45 UTC), quotes Peskov telling journalists that a first tanker had departed for buyers and that further shipments would follow. His answer refers to prior Energy Ministry reports; it verifies an attributed departure statement, not independently inspected vessel records.

[Reuters, carried by MarketScreener October 23](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/russian-diesel-heads-to-us-after-putin-trump-deal-data-shows-ce785cded08bfe2c), reports LSEG data identifying Malta-flagged Agioi Fanendes, 40,000 metric tons of ultra-low-sulfur diesel loaded at Primorsk October 22, a New York destination and expected November 7 arrival. The full indexed Reuters republication was inspected; direct MarketScreener retrieval returned 403 and was not retried. Underlying LSEG records were not obtained. [AP](https://apnews.com/article/f2c733604fd4fbd82e7b668c94fad978) separately attributes New York/November 7 signaling to Kpler, but shared AIS inputs may mean these are not fully independent tracking confirmations. The two wire services' republications are not extra sources.

This advances the record from legal permission and announced volumes to an officially claimed departure supported by attributed shipping-data reporting. It does not establish U.S. arrival, customs entry, offloading, buyer identity, government procurement, settled payment, tax receipts, licensing compliance, complete promised volumes or actual consumer-price effects. Reported 40,000 tons is one cargo, not the announced initial 300,000-ton allocation; November 7 is an estimate, not delivery. Do not add cargo value to any financial-flow total without contracts and settlement evidence.
