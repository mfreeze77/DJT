# October 27 Extreme Networks enforcement resolution

**J20-2026-058 / CH7-EXTREME-ENFORCEMENT-001.** Announcement October 27, 2026; underlying agreements executed earlier that month. This is agency action concerning historical conduct, not a newly stopped export or a personal presidential directive.

## Original instruments and dates

**EXTREME-S001:** [OFAC signed settlement](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937286/download?inline), eleven pages; company signature October 21 and OFAC signature October 22 (p. 11). [October 27 announcement](https://ofac.treasury.gov/recent-actions/20261027) and [enforcement release](https://ofac.treasury.gov/media/937281/download?inline) are **EXTREME-S002**.

**EXTREME-S003:** [DOJ non-prosecution agreement and attachments](https://www.justice.gov/opa/media/1468856/dl?inline), 38 physical pages; letter dated October 20, company signed October 20, counsel and prosecutors October 21 (physical p. 10). [October 27 DOJ announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/national-security-division-announces-first-near-miss-non-prosecution-agreement-under) is **EXTREME-S004**. The coordinated agency releases do not constitute independent corroboration of each other's investigation.

OFAC resolves nine apparent violations during September 2018–March 2022 under 31 CFR 589.201; its agreement is neither a final agency violation determination nor an admission (p. 9, §3). The DOJ agreement, by contrast, expressly accepts responsibility and admits the attached facts and specified violations (p. 3, §4). It is not a conviction or trial judgment. These different legal forms must remain distinct.

## Financial and count reconciliation

OFAC p. 3 specifies a $1,529,685 settlement, credits $487,861 for DOJ-related payment, and requires $1,041,824 payable to Treasury. DOJ specifies a $487,860.92 fine and $487,860.92 forfeiture, totaling $975,721.84. The stated obligations after credit total **$2,017,545.84**, not the sum of both headline amounts and not confirmed cash received. Preserve the documents' rounded credit versus cents-level DOJ figures. OFAC requires payment within 15 days after both signatures; DOJ within ten business days of full execution. No payment record was inspected.

DOJ Attachment A §38 and footnotes 2–3 (physical p. 24) distinguish eleven purchase orders: nine revenue-yielding and two zero-revenue subscriptions. Eight repair/maintenance transactions are separate. Do not add OFAC's nine to DOJ's eleven as twenty exports. Historical proceeds and matching fine/forfeiture figures are not additional historical sales. The revenue-yielding-order dates and OFAC's broader dealings window are not interchangeable.

## Cumulative assessment and beneficiaries

The executed resolutions impose financial and compliance obligations, including DOJ's three-year term and annual reporting and OFAC's five-year certification schedule. These are concrete **US/allied institutional accountability measures**, with prospective deterrence and compliance benefits. They are adverse to the historical Russian military-supply channel, but do not establish a present Russian production loss, new interdiction or collected penalty. The company receives reduced prosecution exposure and cooperation credit while bearing obligations.
